# Auxiliary localization matrices can realize every rank

Status: written proof from elementary elliptic-curve and Galois theory,
with Chebotarev as the one prime-existence theorem. Not Lean-checked.
The seed application imports its established basis and all-odd
residual-image theorem. No Sha finiteness hypothesis is used.

## Theorem F1

Let E/Q be an elliptic curve, p an odd prime, and P1,P2 rational points
whose classes are independent in E(Q)/pE(Q). Suppose the action on
V=E[p] has image GL_2(F_p).

For every matrix M in M_2(F_p), there exist distinct auxiliary primes
ell_1,ell_2, outside any prescribed finite set, satisfying:

1. E has good reduction at ell_i and ell_i differs from p.
2. ell_i is 1 modulo p and E(F_ell_i)[p] has dimension one.
3. There are isomorphisms
   \[
   \iota_i:E(\mathbf F_{\ell_i})/pE(\mathbf F_{\ell_i})
       \longrightarrow\mathbf F_p
   \]
   with \(\iota_i(\overline{P_j})=M_{ij}\).

Each prescribed row is realized at infinitely many auxiliary primes.
Thus invertible matrices and matrices of ranks one and zero all occur.
Without chosen target coordinates, the statement is realization up to
independent nonzero row scalings.

The theorem does not require good reduction at p itself. It does not
prescribe the exact order of E(F_ell_i)[p^\infty].

## Proof

Put K=Q(E[p]), choose Qi with pQi=Pi, and put L=K(Q1,Q2).
The field L is finite Galois over Q: every conjugate of Qi is Qi plus
a p-torsion point. The action embeds Gal(L/Q) into
\[
V^2\rtimes G,\qquad G=\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf F_p),
\]
by
\[
\sigma\longmapsto
 ((\sigma Q_1-Q_1,\sigma Q_2-Q_2),\sigma|_V).
\]
Write H=Gal(L/K), regarded as a G-stable subspace of V^2.

**Full translation image.** The algebra generated by G is M_2(F_p):
subtracting I from the two elementary unipotents gives the off-diagonal
matrix units, and their products give the diagonal units. Consequently,
viewing V^2 as 2 by 2 matrices with G acting on the left, H consists of
matrices with both rows in a single subspace W of F_p^2. To see this
directly, the diagonal matrix units isolate either row, and the
off-diagonal ones move either isolated row to the other position.

If H is proper, W is proper. Choose a nonzero vector (c1,c2) annihilating
W. With integer lifts of the ci, the point Q=c1Q1+c2Q2 is fixed by H,
so belongs to E(K), and pQ=c1P1+c2P2 belongs to E(Q).

For g in G let f(g)=gQ-Q in V. This is a cocycle. With the central
element z=-I, the two expansions of f(zg)=f(gz) give
\[
(z-1)f(g)=(g-1)f(z).
\]
Since z-1=-2 is invertible on V, setting
\(w=(z-1)^{-1}f(z)\) gives \(f(g)=(g-1)w\).
Therefore Q-w is Q-rational and
\[
c_1P_1+c_2P_2=p(Q-w),
\]
contradicting independence modulo p. Hence H=V^2. The projection to G is
surjective, so the entire affine semidirect product is realized: for
each A in G, a lift exists and its translations can be changed
arbitrarily by H.

**Prescribing a row.** Fix
\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its coinvariant quotient V/(U-1)V is one-dimensional. For any desired
row (a1,a2), choose translations ti whose classes are ai in this
quotient. The preceding paragraph supplies an element
\(g=((t_1,t_2),U)\) of Gal(L/Q).

Chebotarev supplies infinitely many unramified primes with Frobenius
conjugate to g, after any finite exclusions. At such a good prime ell,
the determinant on V is ell mod p by the Weil pairing, so ell=1 mod p.
The kernel of U-1 has dimension one; good reduction away from p
identifies this kernel with E(F_ell)[p].

**Identifying the row with reduction of the rational points.**
For arithmetic Frobenius F on the reduced curve, the finite-field
Kummer map is
\[
\kappa:E(\mathbf F_\ell)/pE(\mathbf F_\ell)
 \longrightarrow V/(F-1)V,\qquad
P\longmapsto (F-1)Q,\quad pQ=P.
\]
Changing Q changes the answer by (F-1)V. Its kernel is zero:
if (F-1)Q=(F-1)v for v in V, then Q-v is F-rational and
P=p(Q-v). It is onto: the nonzero endomorphism F-1 of the elliptic
curve is surjective over the algebraic closure, so for any v in V
choose Q with (F-1)Q=v; then pQ is F-rational.
Thus kappa is an isomorphism.

Reducing the global Qi shows that the two Kummer values are precisely
the classes of ti. This uses the standard compatibility of good
reduction with prime-to-ell torsion and the Galois action.

For completeness, conjugating (t,U) by an affine element (b,A) changes
the linear part to U'=AUA^{-1} and the i-th translation to
\[
A t_i+(1-U')b_i.
\]
The second term vanishes in the U'-coinvariants, and A identifies those
coinvariants with the old ones. Thus the row changes only by a common
nonzero scalar after choosing coordinates. An isomorphism iota_i can
absorb that scalar. Choosing two distinct primes from the two infinite
sets proves the theorem. QED.

[Chebotarev, Theorem 28.9 in Sutherland's 18.785 notes](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2019fa/LectureNotes28.pdf).
For the torsion/Frobenius compatibility:

## Theorem F2: what an invertible point-localization matrix actually does

Suppose now that P1,P2 form a basis of E(Q)/pE(Q). Let
\[
W=E(\mathbf Q)/pE(\mathbf Q),\quad
\mathcal S=\operatorname{Sel}_p(E/\mathbf Q),\quad
T=\bigoplus_{i=1}^2E(\mathbf F_{\ell_i})/p.
\]
For an invertible localization matrix, the point-localization map
\(A:W\to T\) is an isomorphism.

The Selmer localization map \(\lambda:\mathcal S\to T\) restricts to A
on W. This is well-defined by the local Kummer condition and good
reduction at ell_i different from p. Therefore
\[
r=A^{-1}\lambda:\mathcal S\longrightarrow W
\]
is a retraction of the Kummer inclusion W into S. Consequently
\[
\mathcal S=W\oplus\ker\lambda,\qquad
\ker\lambda\cong\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[p].
\tag{F2}
\]
The last isomorphism is the restriction to the kernel of the quotient
map in the standard exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow\mathcal S
\longrightarrow\Sha[p]\longrightarrow0.
\]
Explicitly, subtracting r(s) from a lift s of a Sha class gives its
unique representative in ker(lambda). No primary finiteness is assumed.
QED.

An invertible rational-point matrix therefore splits off the known
Mordell–Weil contribution. It does not establish that the remaining
kernel is zero. Requiring that kernel to vanish would be exactly the
original primary obligation, and is not proposed as a new target.

## Seed specialization and relation to earlier work

The retained seed basis and all-odd residual-image theorem imply F1
for every odd p, including p=3. The older LINE audit already proved
existence of invertible core pairs at p>=5, with stronger p-power
conditions. That existence phenomenon is not a new discovery here.

This session gives the written arbitrary-row argument, the explicit
p=3 countercertificate, and the precise failed integer-comparison class
in INTEGER_LIFT_OBSTRUCTION.md. No mathematical novelty claim is made.
