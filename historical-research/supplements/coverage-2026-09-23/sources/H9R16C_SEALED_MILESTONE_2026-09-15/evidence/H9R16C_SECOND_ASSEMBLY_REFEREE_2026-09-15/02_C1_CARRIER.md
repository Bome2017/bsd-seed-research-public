# 02 — C1 (carrier collapse `B_r^bal ≅ R_r`)  [SECOND referee, independent]

**Verdict: CONFIRMED_WITH_NARROWER_HYPOTHESIS.** The collapse, the freeness and the
`Hom`/`H^1` interchange are PROVED. Three narrowings are mandatory; one printed sub-clause
(the rank identity) is FALSE under the only natural reading of its notation and must be restated.

## §1. Types, written out

* Native auxiliary curve `X^g_s := X_1(N_gp^s)` over `Q`. `𝕋'_{N_gp^s}` := the subring of
  `End_Q(J_1(N_gp^s))` generated by `T'_ℓ (ℓ ∤ N_gp)`, `U'_p`, `U'_ℓ (ℓ | N_g)`, `⟨d⟩`
  (`d ∈ (Z/N_gp^s)^×`). It is **commutative** and is a subgroup of the finitely generated free
  `Z`-module `End_Q(J)`, hence finite free over `Z`.
* `R_s := e'_ord𝕋'_{N_gp^s} ⊗ Z_p`. `e'_ord = lim(U'_p)^{n!}` is an idempotent of `𝕋'⊗Z_p`, so `R_s`
  is a direct summand of the finite free `Z_p`-module `𝕋'_{N_gp^s}⊗Z_p`; a finitely generated
  projective module over the local ring `Z_p` is free. **`R_s` is finite free over `Z_p`.**
* `ρ_r` (frozen §C): `(T'_r)^op → T_r`, `T'_ℓ ↦ T_ℓ`, `U'_p ↦ U_p`, `⟨d⟩ ↦ ⟨d⟩^{-1}`,
  `e'_ord ↦ e_ord`. It is the Poincaré/Rosati transpose: `⟨t y, z⟩ = ⟨y, ρ_r(t)z⟩`.
* `A_r := W_{N_gp^r}^{-1}(U'_p)^r = W_{N_gp^r}(U'_p)^r` (`W^2 = id` in weight 2, A1 conventions),
  an operator on `H^1(X^g_r)`, invertible on the `e'_ord`-part (`W` an automorphism, `U'_p` a unit
  there). Transport (frozen §C): `ρ_r(u')A_r = A_ru'`, i.e. `ι_r(u') = A_r^{-1}ρ_r(u')A_r = u'`.
* Carrier (Lane 4 `01` §6, frozen §C):
  `B^bal_r := (T^{(2),ord}_r ⊗_{Z_p} T^{(3),ord}_r)/⟨(t_2u')⊗t_3 − t_2⊗u't_3⟩`.

## §2. Why the transport is available for **both** auxiliary factors (the g / g^c pressure)

The frozen text warns: "`g^c` is the dual/nebentype-twisted third factor. Do not identify the two raw
geometric Hecke actions naively." That warning is about the **raw** actions, and it is exactly what
`ρ_r` and `A_r` discharge. Written out:

1. Factors 2 and 3 of `Δ_r^ord` sit on the **same** curve. At common level both are `X_1(Np^r)`;
   after the tame push-down of C2 both are `X^g_r = X_1(N_gp^r)`. Hence they carry one and the same
   geometric Hecke ring `𝕋'_{N_gp^r}`. `g` versus `g^c` is a statement about which eigen-projection
   is taken **afterwards**, and about the Galois module (`h^c = h ⊗ ε_h^{-1}`, BCPV (1.7)); it is not
   a second, different ring on factor 3.
2. The plain diagonal `Z = X_{23}` induces the Poincaré pairing of that one curve, so an operator on
   factor 2 crosses to factor 3 as its transpose: `Φ^P(t_2u',t_3) = Φ^P(t_2, ρ_r(u')t_3)` — the frozen
   raw balance. This is an **asymmetric** relation: primed on factor 2, `ρ_r`(primed) on factor 3.
3. `A_r` is inserted on factor 3 only. With `ρ_r(u')A_r = A_ru'`:
   `Φ^mod(t_2u',t_3) = Φ^P(t_2u', A_rt_3) = Φ^P(t_2, ρ_r(u')A_rt_3) = Φ^P(t_2, A_ru't_3) = Φ^mod(t_2,u't_3)`.
   The asymmetry is **removed**: the same primed symbol `u'` acts on both auxiliary factors.
4. **The anti-involution is harmless because the ring is commutative, and only because of that.**
   `ρ_r` is an anti-homomorphism, so `ρ_r(t_2u') = ρ_r(u')ρ_r(t_2)`; step 3 needs
   `ρ_r(u')ρ_r(t_2)A_r = ρ_r(t_2)ρ_r(u')A_r`. On a non-commutative carrier ring this step fails and
   the balance relation would not be middle-linearity. Commutativity of `𝕋'` is therefore a
   **load-bearing hypothesis**, and it is the "commutative primed ordinary Hecke model" of §6 of the
   charter. Stated correctly, the transport is available **for the pair (2,3) jointly**: what is
   transported is the adjoint relation *between* the two factors, not an `A_r` on each of them.

**Numerical confirmation (mine, `_verify/transport_check.py`, `_verify/transport_check.log`).**
Exact weight-2 Manin-symbol computation mod ℓ ∈ {1000003, 999983}, `(N_g,p,r) ∈ {(5,3,1),(7,2,1),(3,5,1)}`,
every operator built from its own definition:
`⟨d⟩^{-1}A_r = A_r⟨d⟩` (4 diamonds per case), `U_pA_r = A_rU'_p`, `W T_ℓ W = ⟨ℓ⟩^{-1}T_ℓ` (so
`ρ_r(T'_ℓ) = T_ℓ`), `T'_ℓU'_p = U'_pT'_ℓ`, `T_ℓA_r = A_rT'_ℓ`, and
`ρ_r(t_2)A_rt_3 = A_rt_2t_3` for random products `t_2,t_3` in `⟨U'_p, T'_ℓ, ⟨d⟩⟩`. All PASS.

## §3. The collapse

Because `ρ_r(t_2)A_r = A_rt_2`, the operator attached to `(t_2,t_3)` is
`ρ_r(t_2)A_rt_3 = A_r·t_2t_3`: **it depends on `(t_2,t_3)` only through the product `t_2t_3 ∈ R_r`.**

Algebraically: with `T^{(2),ord}_r = T^{(3),ord}_r = R_r` (the regular module over itself — §4.1),
the printed relation `(t_2u')⊗t_3 − t_2⊗u't_3`, for `u'` ranging over a `Z_p`-algebra generating set
of `R_r`, generates exactly the middle-linearity submodule: if the relation holds for `u'_1,u'_2`
then `(t_2u'_1u'_2)⊗t_3 ∼ (t_2u'_1)⊗u'_2t_3 ∼ t_2⊗u'_1u'_2t_3`, and `Z_p`-linearity is automatic.
Hence

    B^bal_r = R_r ⊗_{R_r} R_r  ≅  R_r ,      [t_2⊗t_3] ↦ t_2t_3 ,

with inverse `t ↦ [t⊗1] = [1⊗t]`. (`R_r` commutative, so left/right regular structures agree.)
Therefore `B^bal_r` is **finite free over `Z_p`**, of rank `rank_{Z_p}R_r`. This answers Lane 4
`03` §6.3's standing worry ("`B^bal_r` is a quotient module and may have torsion"): it is not — but
only after the collapse is proved, and the proof of the collapse uses no freeness. **No circularity.**

The `R_r`-module structure is the one used later: `1⊗N_{r+1,r}`, `(U'_p)^{-2s}_{carrier}` and
`res^bal` are all multiplication by, resp. the ring map applied to, elements of `R_s`. In particular
`1⊗ν` and `ν⊗1` become the *same* map after the collapse — C4 silently uses this.

## §4. The three mandatory narrowings

**4.1 Level.** `R_r` must be the **native**-level algebra `e'_ord𝕋'_{N_gp^r}⊗Z_p`, as C1 writes it —
never the common-level `e'_ord𝕋'_{Np^r}⊗Z_p` of Lane 2 `01` T3 ("`M_r := 𝕋'^{(2)}_r ⊗_Z 𝕋'^{(3)}_r`"
with `𝕋'_r ⊂ End_Q(J_1(Np^r))`). Reason: the collapse runs through `ρ_r(u')A_r = A_ru'`, and `A_r`
is an operator at native level `N_gp^r`; a common-level `U'_ℓ` for `ℓ | t = N/N_g` is not intertwined
by the tame push-down with any native-level operator, so the relation is not even typed for it.
With C1's own `R_r` this is correct; Lane 2's looser wording is the one to discard.

**4.2 Both auxiliary coefficient modules are the regular `R_r`-module.** C1's `B^bal_r = R_r⊗_{R_r}R_r`
presupposes `T^{(2),ord}_r = T^{(3),ord}_r = R_r`. This is legitimate for the H9R16-A/B carrier (its
generators *are* the Hecke pairs `(t_2,t_3)`), but it is an additional pinning: Lane 4 `01` §6 leaves
`T^{(2),ord}_r`, `T^{(3),ord}_r` undefined. Under any other choice (e.g. a module of test vectors, or
a quotient by an eigen-ideal) the conclusion is `T^{(2)} ⊗_{R_r} T^{(3)}`, which is neither `R_r` nor
automatically free.

**4.3 The `Hom`/`H^1` interchange is for a Galois-trivial carrier.** `Hom_{Z_p}(B^bal_r, H^1(F,T)) =
H^1(F, Hom_{Z_p}(B^bal_r,T))` holds because `B^bal_r ≅ Z_p^{n}` finite free forces
`Hom_{Z_p}(B^bal_r,T) ≅ T^{⊕n}` **as `G_F`-modules** and `H^1(F,−)` commutes with finite direct sums.
That isomorphism of Galois modules needs `G_F` to act trivially on the carrier. It does for
`F ⊇ Q(μ_N)`, in particular for `F = F'_r` and `F = F'_{r+1}`: for `σ ∈ G_{F'_s}`, `n_σ ≡ 1 (N)` so
`R(⟨n_σ⟩) = 1`, and `m_σ ∈ 1+p^sZ_p` so `⟨m_σ⟩^{±1/2} = 1` at level `N_fp^s`; the `⋆`-action of
`G_{F'_s}` is the plain action. **The interchange must NOT be applied to the `⋆`-twisted module over
`K_0`**, where the carrier action `R(⟨n_σ⟩)` is non-trivial and `T^♮_r ≇ T^{⊕n}` as a `G_{K_0}`-module.
C3 and C4 respect this (they state their identities over `F'_r`, `F'_{r+1}`), so nothing breaks; but
C1's clause as printed carries no field hypothesis and would be false if read over `K_0`.

## §5. The rank clause: FALSE as printed

C1: "`rank_{Z_p}T_r^♮ = rank_{Z_p}(R_r)·2g^{(1)}_r` with no `|G_r|` factor."

With `T^♮_r = Hom_{Z_p}(B^bal_r, T_pJ^{(1),ord}_r)` (C3's own definition) the correct identity is

    rank_{Z_p} T^♮_r = rank_{Z_p}(R_r) · rank_{Z_p}( e'_ord T_pJ^{(1)}_r ).

`e'_ordT_pJ^{(1)}_r` is a **proper** direct summand of `T_pJ^{(1)}_r`: by Hida's control theorem the
ordinary part grows like `p^{r}·const` while `2g^{(1)}_r ≍ p^{2r}`, so for `r ≥ 2`
`rank(e'_ordT_pJ^{(1)}_r) < 2g^{(1)}_r` and the printed equality fails. The only reading under which
it is true is `g^{(1)}_r :=` the dimension of the ordinary part of `J^{(1)}_r` — a reading the claims
file nowhere licenses (`g` is elsewhere the auxiliary Hida family, and the superscript `(1)` is the
factor index, so `g^{(1)}_r` reads as the genus of `X_1(N_fp^r)`).

**Scope of the error: nil.** The clause's operative content — *no `|G_r| = p^{3(r−1)}` factor* — is
correct and is what C6 needs; the second factor is never used numerically anywhere in C2–C8. This is
a mis-stated bookkeeping identity inside C1, not a defect of the assembly.
