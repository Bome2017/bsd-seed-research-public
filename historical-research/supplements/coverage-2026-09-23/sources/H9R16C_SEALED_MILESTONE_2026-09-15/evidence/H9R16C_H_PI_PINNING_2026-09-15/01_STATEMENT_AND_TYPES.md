# 01 — H_pi: full statement with types

Conventions are those of BCPV §1.1.6–1.1.8 (`BCPV.tex` l.497–499) and DR22 §1.3
(`DR22.tex` l.1140–1225).  `p` is odd, `p ∤ N_g`, `r ≥ 1` throughout.

## §1. Objects

**Curves.**  `X_s := X_1(N_g p^s)` / `Y_s := Y_1(N_g p^s)` over `Q`, in the *point-of-order*
model: a point of `Y_s` is `(A,P)`, `A` an elliptic curve, `P ∈ A` of exact order `N_g p^s`.
This is BCPV's and KLZ2's model (KLZ17 proof of `thm:ordinaryfiltration`, `KLZ17.tex` l.2286:
"Ohta uses the notation `Y_1(N)` for the curve classifying embeddings of `μ_N`, rather than a
point of order `N`").  `J_s := Jac(X_s)`.

**Degeneracy maps.**  DR22 (1.3), `DR22.tex` l.1146–1155:

    ϖ_1 : X_{r+1} → X_r ,  (A,P) ↦ (A, pP)          [τ ↦ τ on H]
    ϖ_2 : X_{r+1} → X_r ,  (A,P) ↦ (A/⟨p^r P⟩, P mod ⟨p^r P⟩)   [τ ↦ pτ on H]

`ϖ_1` is the covering attached to `Γ_1(N_g p^{r+1}) ⊂ Γ_1(N_g p^r)`.  For `r ≥ 1` it is
finite flat of degree `p^2`, Galois, with group
`K_r := ker((Z/N_g p^{r+1})^× → (Z/N_g p^r)^×) ≅ Z/p` acting through the diamonds
(DR22 l.1142–1145: `⟨a⟩(A,P) = (A,aP)`; `X_r → X_{0,r}` is Galois with group `(Z/p^r)^×`).
Note `|K_r| = p` while `deg ϖ_1 = p^2`: `ϖ_1` is Galois as a map of *coarse* curves only
after the `±1` identification; the numerically relevant statement, verified in `04`, is
`ϖ_{1,*} ϖ_1^* = p^2`.

**Carriers of the cohomology.**  Three modules are in play and must be kept apart:

| symbol | definition | `Z_p`-structure |
|---|---|---|
| `S_s`  | `H^1_ét(X̄_s, Z_p(1))` (compact model; Lane 2 `01`) | free, rank `2g(X_s)` |
| `S^o_s`| `H^1_ét(Ȳ_s, Z_p(1))` (open model; BCPV l.497) | free, finite rank |
| `Ĵ_s`  | `J_s(k) ⊗ Z_p` (Mordell–Weil; Hida's `Ĵ_r`) | f.g. `Z_p`-module, may have torsion |

`S_s` and `S^o_s` are **torsion-free**: for the compact model `H^1_ét(X̄_s,Z_p(1)) ≅
Hom(T_p J_s, Z_p)(1)`; for the open model `H^1_ét(Ȳ_s, Z/p^n)` is free over `Z/p^n`, and the
limit is `Z_p`-free.  `Ĵ_s` is **not** torsion-free in general — this is why the intertwining
must be asserted on `S_s` (or `S^o_s`) and only then transported to `Ĵ_s`.

**Hecke operators (primed = "star" = Albanese/`pr_*`-normalised).**  KLZ17 Definition at
`KLZ17.tex` l.577–579:  `T'_ℓ = pr_* ∘ (Φ_ℓ)^* ∘ hpr^*` (`ℓ ∤ MN`), `U'_ℓ` the same formula
for `ℓ | MN`; `T_ℓ` is its Poincaré-transpose ("it is the `T_ℓ` rather than the `T'_ℓ` that
correspond to the familiar formulae for the action on `q`-expansions", `KLZ17.tex` l.581).
Diamonds `⟨d⟩`, `d ∈ (Z/N_g p^s)^×`, act through `(A,P) ↦ (A,dP)`.

**Hecke rings.**  Let `A_s := Z[ X_ℓ (ℓ∤N_gp), X_{U_p}, X_{U_ℓ} (ℓ|N_g), Y_d (d ∈ (Z/N_gp^s)^×) ]`
be the free (commutative) polynomial ring on abstract generators, and
`ev_s : A_s → End_{Z_p}(S_s ⊗ Z_p)` the evaluation `X_ℓ ↦ T'_ℓ`, `X_{U_p} ↦ U'_p`,
`X_{U_ℓ} ↦ U'_ℓ`, `Y_d ↦ ⟨d⟩`.  Then

    T'_{N_g p^s} := im(ev_s)  ⊂ End(S_s),      R_s := e'_ord · (T'_{N_g p^s} ⊗ Z_p) ⊂ End(e'_ord S_s),

with `e'_ord := lim_n (U'_p)^{n!}` (BCPV l.499).  `R_s` is a finite `Z_p`-algebra, hence
`p`-adically complete, and `e'_ord` is a central idempotent of `T'_{N_gp^s} ⊗ Z_p`, so
`R_s = (T'_{N_gp^s} ⊗ Z_p)/(1−e'_ord)` is a **quotient ring** of `T'_{N_gp^s} ⊗ Z_p`.
(The same definitions with `S^o_s` in place of `S_s` give BCPV's ring; everything below is
proved for either carrier, and `04` verifies the modular-symbol carrier
`MS_2(Γ_1(N_gp^s))`, which is the `Z`-dual of `H^1(Y_s, Z)` = the open model.)

*Redundancy of the diamonds.*  `ℓ⟨ℓ⟩ = T(ℓ)^2 − T(ℓ^2)` for `ℓ ∤ N_g p`
(Hida, *Control of Λ-adic MW groups*, §1, p.3, printed for the unprimed operators; the
primed identity is its Poincaré transpose).  Since `ℓ ∈ Z_p^×` for `ℓ ≠ p` and every class in
`(Z/N_gp^s)^×` contains such a prime, `R_s` is already generated over `Z_p` by
`{T'_ℓ, U'_p, U'_ℓ}`.  This matches KLZ17 (`𝕋_{Np^∞}` "generated by the operators `T'_n` for
all `n ≥ 1`", `KLZ17.tex` l.2251) and BCPV (which lists the diamonds redundantly, l.499).

## §2. The statement H_pi, in three graded forms

> **H_pi(coh)** — *generator intertwining on cohomology.*  As maps `S_{r+1} → S_r` (resp.
> `S^o_{r+1} → S^o_r`), integrally:
>
> | (G1) | `ϖ_{1,*} ∘ T'_ℓ = T'_ℓ ∘ ϖ_{1,*}` | `ℓ ∤ N_g p` |
> | (G2) | `ϖ_{1,*} ∘ U'_p = U'_p ∘ ϖ_{1,*}` | |
> | (G3) | `ϖ_{1,*} ∘ U'_ℓ = U'_ℓ ∘ ϖ_{1,*}` | `ℓ | N_g` |
> | (G4) | `ϖ_{1,*} ∘ ⟨d̃⟩_{r+1} = ⟨d̃ mod N_g p^r⟩_r ∘ ϖ_{1,*}` | `d̃ ∈ (Z/N_gp^{r+1})^×` |
>
> **H_pi(ring)** — *existence.*  There is a ring homomorphism `π_r : R_{r+1} → R_r` with
> `ϖ_{1,*} ∘ t = π_r(t) ∘ ϖ_{1,*}` in `Hom_{Z_p}(e'S_{r+1}, e'S_r)` for every `t ∈ R_{r+1}`.
>
> **H_pi(surj)** — `π_r` is surjective.

The consumers (Lane 2 `03`, second referee `05` §1, §6) use exactly: `H_pi(ring)` in Lemma (L)
and in moving `res^bal` past `(U'_p)^{-2(r+1)}`, and `H_pi(surj)` in C8 for injectivity of
`π_r^*`.

## §3. What each clause *needs* — the type analysis

### 3.1 Where the intertwining lives
`ϖ_{1,*}` is a map **between different modules** (`S_{r+1} → S_r`), so `ϖ_{1,*}t = π_r(t)ϖ_{1,*}`
is an equation in `Hom_{Z_p}(e'S_{r+1}, e'S_r)`, *not* a commutator identity in one ring.  In
particular H_pi is **not** a statement that `ϖ_{1,*}` is Hecke-equivariant for one action; it is
the statement that the level-`(r+1)` action *descends along* `ϖ_{1,*}`.

`e'_ord` is a limit of powers of `U'_p`, so (G2) alone already gives
`ϖ_{1,*} ∘ e'_{r+1} = e'_r ∘ ϖ_{1,*}`, i.e. `ϖ_{1,*}` carries `e'S_{r+1}` into `e'S_r` and the
whole statement restricts to the ordinary parts.  **Ordinarity is therefore not a hypothesis of
H_pi(coh) and not a hypothesis of the existence proof** (see `03`); it is only the operation that
cuts the statement down to the module the consumers use.

The Mordell–Weil form is a *corollary*, not the statement: `ϖ_{1,*}` on `Ĵ` is Albanese
functoriality `ϖ_{1,*} : J_{r+1} → J_r`, and (G1)–(G4) on `J` push to `Ĵ(k) = J(k)⊗Z_p`
functorially.  Torsion in `Ĵ` does not obstruct this direction; it obstructs only *uniqueness*
(§3.3), which is why uniqueness must be proved on `S`, not on `Ĵ`.

### 3.2 Well-definedness: the generators do NOT suffice
The assignment `T'_ℓ ↦ T'_ℓ`, `U'_p ↦ U'_p`, `U'_ℓ ↦ U'_ℓ`, `⟨d̃⟩ ↦ ⟨d̃ mod N_gp^r⟩` is a
homomorphism `φ_r : A_{r+1} → A_r` of the *free* rings.  For it to descend to
`π_r : R_{r+1} → R_r` one needs

    (WD)   ker( A_{r+1} → R_{r+1} )  ⊆  ker( A_{r+1} --φ_r--> A_r → R_r ),

i.e. **every polynomial relation among the level-`(r+1)` operators must already hold among the
level-`r` operators**.  This is a genuine hypothesis: (G1)–(G4) are statements about `ϖ_{1,*}`,
and `ϖ_{1,*}` is *not injective*, so they transport nothing backwards by themselves.
The inclusion (WD) goes in the direction "more level ⟹ more relations hold at less level",
which is the direction supplied by an **injection of the small carrier into the big one**, not by
a surjection of the big onto the small.  `03` supplies exactly such an injection.

Two honest routes to (WD), both used in `03`:
* **(WD-inj)** an injective `Z_p`-linear `ι : e'S_r ↪ e'S_{r+1}` with `g_{r+1} ∘ ι = ι ∘ φ_r(g)_r`
  for every generator `g`.  Then `ev_{r+1}(P) = 0 ⟹ ι ∘ ev_r(φ_r P) = 0 ⟹ ev_r(φ_r P) = 0`.
* **(WD-ctrl)** the Hida–Ohta control theorem: `e'S_r` is a quotient of `e'S_{r+1}` **as a module
  over one big algebra** `𝕋_{N_gp^∞}` in which both `R_r` and `R_{r+1}` are quotients with the
  generators matching; then `Ann(e'S_{r+1}) ⊆ Ann(e'S_r)` and (WD) follows.

### 3.3 Uniqueness of `π_r(t)`
Given `t`, an element `s ∈ R_r` with `ϖ_{1,*}t = s ϖ_{1,*}` is unique **iff** no nonzero
`s ∈ R_r` kills `im(ϖ_{1,*}|_{e'S_{r+1}})`.  Sufficient (in increasing strength):

* **(U1) finite index.** `ϖ_{1,*}ϖ_1^* = deg(ϖ_1) = p^2` (verified in `04`, check `(i)`), so
  `im(ϖ_{1,*}) ⊇ p^2 e'S_r`.  Since `e'S_r` is `Z_p`-**torsion-free** (§1), `s·p^2 e'S_r = 0`
  forces `s = 0` in `End(e'S_r)`, hence in `R_r ⊂ End(e'S_r)`.  Uniqueness holds
  unconditionally.  Note this argument **fails verbatim on `Ĵ_r`**, which has torsion: there one
  only gets uniqueness up to an operator killing `Ĵ_r[p^∞]`.
* **(U2) surjectivity.**  `e'ϖ_{1,*} : e'S_{r+1} ↠ e'S_r` is onto — this is Ohta (1.2.12)
  (see `02`).  Then `s` is determined on all of `e'S_r` directly.

So the prompt's remark is correct in substance ("uniqueness needs `ϖ_{1,*}` to be surjective or
to have dense enough image"), and the *weak* form (U1) already suffices and is elementary;
(U2) is the printed sharpening.  **Uniqueness is never the obstruction; well-definedness is.**

### 3.4 Surjectivity
`R_r` is generated as a `Z_p`-algebra by `T'_ℓ, U'_p, U'_ℓ, ⟨d⟩`, `d ∈ (Z/N_gp^r)^×`.  Each is
in the image of `φ_r` (for the diamonds because `(Z/N_gp^{r+1})^× ↠ (Z/N_gp^r)^×`).  So
**given** (WD), `H_pi(surj)` is automatic.  Surjectivity is therefore *not* an extra analytic
input; it is a corollary of well-definedness plus the (trivial) surjectivity of the reduction of
diamond indices.  Note `π_r` is surjective but **never injective** for `r ≥ 1`
(`rank_{Z_p} R_{r+1} > rank_{Z_p} R_r`; see the ranks in `04`).

### 3.5 Integrality
Every clause above is asserted over `Z_p` with no denominators.  Nothing in H_pi requires
inverting `p`: the only division that appears anywhere in the vicinity is `(U'_p)^{-1}`, which
exists **integrally** on `e'_ord`-parts by definition of `e'_ord`.  See `05`.

## §4. The statement actually to be proved in `03`

> **(H_pi).** Let `p` be odd, `p ∤ N_g`, `r ≥ 1`.  Then (G1)–(G4) hold integrally on
> `H^1_ét(X̄_•, Z_p(1))` and on `H^1_ét(Ȳ_•, Z_p(1))`; the assignment `φ_r` descends to a
> surjective homomorphism of `Z_p`-algebras `π_r : T'_{N_gp^{r+1}}⊗Z_p ↠ T'_{N_gp^r}⊗Z_p`, and
> `π_r(e'_{r+1}) = e'_r`, so `π_r` restricts to a surjection `R_{r+1} ↠ R_r`; and for every
> `t ∈ R_{r+1}`, `π_r(t)` is the **unique** element of `R_r` with `ϖ_{1,*}t = π_r(t)ϖ_{1,*}`.
