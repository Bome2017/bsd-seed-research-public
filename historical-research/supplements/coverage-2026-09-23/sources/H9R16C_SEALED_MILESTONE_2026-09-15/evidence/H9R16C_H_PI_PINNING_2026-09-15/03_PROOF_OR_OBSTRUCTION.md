# 03 — Proof of H_pi

**Result of this file: H_pi is PROVED, integrally, including surjectivity, and without any
ordinarity hypothesis.**  The proof is short once the right injection is identified.  The
identification is the whole content: `ϖ_{1,*}` carries the *primed* Hecke ring forward, and the map
that carries it *backward* — the one that makes the assignment on generators a well-defined ring map
— is **`ϖ_2^*`, not `ϖ_1^*`**.  `ϖ_1^*` carries the *unprimed* ring.  This dichotomy is the reason
H_pi is not a one-line citation of Hida's tower (whose printed transition map is restriction along
`ϖ_1^*`, for the unprimed algebra: `02` §4), and it is confirmed numerically in `04` by three
predicted-negative controls.

Throughout: `p` odd, `p ∤ N_g`, `r ≥ 1`, `M := N_g p^r`.  `S_s` is either
`H^1_ét(X̄_1(N_gp^s), Z_p(1))` or `H^1_ét(Ȳ_1(N_gp^s), Z_p(1))`; both are finite free `Z_p`-modules
(`01` §1) and the proof is identical for the two.  `𝕋'_s ⊂ End_{Z_p}(S_s)` is the `Z_p`-subalgebra
generated by `T'_ℓ (ℓ∤N_gp)`, `U'_p`, `U'_ℓ (ℓ|N_g)`, `⟨d⟩ (d ∈ (Z/N_gp^s)^×)`; it is a finite
`Z_p`-module (a `Z_p`-submodule of the finite free module `End_{Z_p}(S_s)`, `Z_p` noetherian), hence
`p`-adically complete and separated.  `R_s := e'_ord 𝕋'_s`.

## §1. Step 1 — the generator intertwinings (G1)–(G4) for `ϖ_{1,*}`

### 1.1 (G4), diamonds — an identity of morphisms of curves
For `d̃ ∈ (Z/N_gp^{r+1})^×`, `⟨d̃⟩(A,P) = (A, d̃P)` (DR22 l.1142–1145).  Then

    ϖ_1(⟨d̃⟩(A,P)) = ϖ_1(A, d̃P) = (A, p d̃ P) = (A, d̃ (pP)) = ⟨d̃ mod N_gp^r⟩(ϖ_1(A,P)),

the last equality because at level `N_gp^r` the automorphism `⟨d̃⟩` depends only on `d̃ mod N_gp^r`.
So `ϖ_1 ∘ ⟨d̃⟩_{r+1} = ⟨d̃ mod N_gp^r⟩_r ∘ ϖ_1` **as morphisms of curves over `Q`**, and functoriality
of proper pushforward gives (G4) on `S_•`.  *(Whether `⟨d⟩` acts on cohomology as `⟨d⟩_*` or as
`(⟨d⟩^{-1})^*` is immaterial: both are functorial.)*  ∎

### 1.2 (G1) and (G3), `ℓ ≠ p` — a cartesian square
Let `ℓ ≠ p` be a prime and `L` a level.  Write `Y_1(L)_ℓ` for the moduli of triples `(A,P,C)` with
`P` of exact order `L` and `C ⊂ A` cyclic of order `ℓ` (subject, when `ℓ | L`, to `C ∩ ⟨P⟩ = 0`),
with the two projections

    q_1(A,P,C) = (A,P),     q_2(A,P,C) = (A/C, P mod C).

`T'_ℓ` (`ℓ∤L`) and `U'_ℓ` (`ℓ|L`) are `q_{1,*} ∘ Φ ∘ q_2^*` for the canonical twist `Φ` on the
coefficient sheaf (KLZ17 Definition, `KLZ17.tex` l.577–579; the twist acts on `∧²H ≅ Z_p(1)` as
multiplication by `ℓ`, `KLZ17.tex` l.589, and is therefore a **level-independent scalar**).

Define `ϖ_1^{(ℓ)} : Y_1(N_gp^{r+1})_ℓ → Y_1(N_gp^r)_ℓ` by `(A,P,C) ↦ (A,pP,C)`.  Note the side
condition matches: for `|C| = ℓ ≠ p` the condition `C ∩ ⟨P⟩ = 0` is about the `ℓ`-part of `⟨P⟩`,
which is the same for `P` and for `pP` (`r ≥ 1`, `ℓ ∤ p`).  Then:

* **Square for `q_1`.**  `q_1 ∘ ϖ_1^{(ℓ)} = ϖ_1 ∘ q_1`.  It is cartesian: the fibre product
  `Y_1(N_gp^{r+1}) ×_{Y_1(N_gp^r)} Y_1(N_gp^r)_ℓ` is `{(A,P̃,C)}` with `P̃` of order `N_gp^{r+1}` and
  `C` an `ℓ`-subgroup of the *same* `A`, i.e. `Y_1(N_gp^{r+1})_ℓ`.
* **Square for `q_2`.**  `q_2 ∘ ϖ_1^{(ℓ)}(A,P̃,C) = (A/C, pP̃ mod C) = ϖ_1(A/C, P̃ mod C) =
  ϖ_1 ∘ q_2(A,P̃,C)`.  Cartesian: given `(A,P,C) ∈ Y_1(N_gp^r)_ℓ` and `Q ∈ A/C` with `pQ = P mod C`,
  the isogeny `A → A/C` has degree `ℓ` prime to `p`, hence is an **isomorphism on `p`-power torsion
  cosets**: `P̃ ↦ P̃ mod C` is a bijection `{P̃ ∈ A : pP̃ = P} → {Q ∈ A/C : pQ = P mod C}` (both are
  torsors under `A[p] ≅ (A/C)[p]`).  So there is a unique lift.

Proper base change / the projection formula for the cartesian squares then gives, on `S_•`,

    ϖ_{1,*} ∘ (q_{1,*} Φ q_2^*)_{r+1}  =  (q_{1,*} Φ q_2^*)_r ∘ ϖ_{1,*},

i.e. (G1) for `ℓ ∤ N_gp` and (G3) for `ℓ | N_g`.  ∎

*(Remark: the same two squares with `ϖ_1` replaced by `ϖ_2` are cartesian by the identical argument —
`ϖ_2` is an isogeny of degree `p`, again prime to `ℓ` — which will give the `ℓ ≠ p` half of Step 2
without appeal to `W`.)*

### 1.3 (G2), `U'_p` — PRINTED
`ϖ_{1,*} U'_p = U'_p ϖ_{1,*}` is **DR22 §1.3**, `DR22.tex` l.1220–1222 ("The operator `U_p^*` is
compatible with the transition maps defining `H^1_ét(X̄^*_∞,Z_p)`") and l.1569 ("Since `U_p^*`
commutes with the push-forward maps `ϖ_{1*}`"), with `U_p^* = π_{1*}π_2^*` their (= our) `U'_p`
(l.1216).  The prompt lists it as printed; it is re-verified numerically in `04`.  It is **not**
provable by the base-change argument of §1.2: the `q_2`-square for `ℓ = p` is *not* cartesian
(`P̃ ↦ P̃ mod C` is then `p`-to-`1` onto a proper subset), which is exactly why DR22 have to state it
separately, and why KLZ17 `prop:pridentity1` has a correction term in the `p ∤ N` regime.  ∎

**Conclusion of Step 1.**  (G1)–(G4) hold, integrally, on `S_{r+1} → S_r`.  Since
`e'_ord = lim_n (U'_p)^{n!}` in the `p`-adically complete ring `End_{Z_p}(S_s)`, (G2) gives
`ϖ_{1,*} e'_{r+1} = e'_r ϖ_{1,*}`, so all of (G1)–(G4) restrict to `e'S_{r+1} → e'S_r`.

## §2. Step 2 — the backward injection: `ϖ_2^*`

**Lemma 2.1 (injectivity).**  `ϖ_1^* : S_r → S_{r+1}` and `ϖ_2^* : S_r → S_{r+1}` are injective.

*Proof.*  `ϖ_i` is finite flat of degree `p^2` (`01` §1), so `ϖ_{i,*}ϖ_i^* = deg(ϖ_i) = p^2` on
`S_r`.  `S_r` is `Z_p`-torsion-free, so `ker(ϖ_i^*) ⊆ ker(p^2) = 0`.  ∎
*(`ϖ_{1,*}ϖ_1^* = p^2` is verified in `04`, check `(deg)`.)*

**Lemma 2.2 (`ϖ_1^*` carries the UNPRIMED ring).**  For every `t` in the list `T_ℓ (ℓ∤N_gp)`, `U_p`,
`U_ℓ (ℓ|N_g)`, `⟨d⟩`, one has `t_{r+1} ∘ ϖ_1^* = ϖ_1^* ∘ t_r` (diamonds: `⟨d̃⟩_{r+1}ϖ_1^* =
ϖ_1^*⟨d̃ mod N_gp^r⟩_r`, for **any** lift `d̃`).

*Proof.*  `T_n` is the Poincaré-transpose of `T'_n` (KLZ17 `KLZ17.tex` l.581) and `ϖ_1^*` is the
adjoint of `ϖ_{1,*}` for the Poincaré pairings (projection formula
`⟨ϖ_{1,*}x, y⟩_r = ⟨x, ϖ_1^*y⟩_{r+1}`).  Take adjoints in (G1)–(G4):
`⟨ϖ_{1,*}T'x, y⟩_r = ⟨T'ϖ_{1,*}x, y⟩_r = ⟨ϖ_{1,*}x, Ty⟩_r = ⟨x, ϖ_1^*Ty⟩_{r+1}` and
`⟨ϖ_{1,*}T'x,y⟩_r = ⟨T'x, ϖ_1^*y⟩_{r+1} = ⟨x, Tϖ_1^*y⟩_{r+1}`; non-degeneracy gives
`ϖ_1^*T_r = T_{r+1}ϖ_1^*`.  For the diamonds, the adjoint of `⟨d⟩` is `⟨d⟩^{-1}`, and the
independence of the lift `d̃` also follows directly from `ϖ_1 ∘ ⟨k⟩_{r+1} = ϖ_1` for `k ∈
ker((Z/N_gp^{r+1})^× → (Z/N_gp^r)^×)`.  ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (`W`-conjugation).**  Let `W_L` be the Atkin–Lehner/Fricke involution of `X_1(L)`,
regarded only as a `Z_p`-module automorphism of the **geometric** cohomology `S(L)` (it is defined
over `Q(μ_L)`; `H_pi` is a statement about `Z_p`-linear endomorphisms, so no Galois-equivariance is
used).  Then
  (a) `W_L T'_n W_L^{-1} = T_n` and `W_L ⟨d⟩ W_L^{-1} = ⟨d⟩^{-1}` — Ohta99 **(1.5.4)**, p.252
      ("`H_k(Γ_r;R)` and `H^*_k(Γ_r;R)` are canonically isomorphic via `T(n) ↔ T^*(n)`");
  (b) `ϖ_2^* = W_{N_gp^{r+1}} ∘ ϖ_1^* ∘ W_{N_gp^r}`.

*Proof of (b).*  On the upper half plane `ϖ_1` is `τ ↦ τ`, `ϖ_2` is `τ ↦ pτ` (DR22 l.1147, l.1155),
and `W_L` is `τ ↦ −1/(Lτ)`.  Hence, as maps `X_1(N_gp^{r+1}) → X_1(N_gp^r)`,

    ϖ_1 ∘ W_{N_gp^{r+1}} : τ ↦ −1/(N_gp^{r+1}τ),
    W_{N_gp^r} ∘ ϖ_2     : τ ↦ pτ ↦ −1/(N_gp^r · pτ) = −1/(N_gp^{r+1}τ),

so `ϖ_1 ∘ W_{r+1} = W_r ∘ ϖ_2`.  Taking pullbacks, `W_{r+1}^* ϖ_1^* = ϖ_2^* W_r^*`; `W` is invertible
(`W_L^2 = ⟨−1⟩`, a diamond, hence a unit), so `ϖ_2^* = W_{r+1}^{*,-1} ϖ_1^* W_r^{*}`, which is (b) in
the normalisation `W^2 = 1` used by the checker.  ∎  *(So (b) is **not** an unproved citation; it is
also Lane 5's Lemma W, checked there as `(iv)/(iv')`, and re-verified here as check `(W)` of `04`.)*

**Proposition 2.4 (`ϖ_2^*` carries the PRIMED ring).**  `ϖ_2^* : S_r ↪ S_{r+1}` is injective and

    T'_{ℓ,r+1} ∘ ϖ_2^* = ϖ_2^* ∘ T'_{ℓ,r},   U'_{p,r+1} ∘ ϖ_2^* = ϖ_2^* ∘ U'_{p,r},
    U'_{ℓ,r+1} ∘ ϖ_2^* = ϖ_2^* ∘ U'_{ℓ,r},   ⟨d̃⟩_{r+1} ∘ ϖ_2^* = ϖ_2^* ∘ ⟨d̃ mod N_gp^r⟩_r .

*Proof.*  Injectivity: Lemma 2.1 (or 2.3(b) with `W` invertible).  Equivariance: conjugate Lemma 2.2
by `W` using 2.3(a),(b).  E.g. for `U'_p`:
`ϖ_2^* U'_{p,r} = W_{r+1}ϖ_1^* W_r U'_{p,r} = W_{r+1}ϖ_1^* U_{p,r} W_r = W_{r+1}U_{p,r+1}ϖ_1^* W_r
= U'_{p,r+1} W_{r+1}ϖ_1^* W_r = U'_{p,r+1}ϖ_2^*`.  For the diamonds the two inversions cancel:
`ϖ_2^*⟨d⟩_r = W_{r+1}ϖ_1^*⟨d^{-1}⟩_r W_r = W_{r+1}⟨d̃^{-1}⟩_{r+1}ϖ_1^* W_r = ⟨d̃⟩_{r+1}ϖ_2^*`.  ∎

*Independent route avoiding `W` (for `ℓ ≠ p`):* the two cartesian squares of §1.2 with `ϖ_2` in place
of `ϖ_1` give `ϖ_2^*` equivariance for `T'_ℓ, U'_ℓ` and (by the morphism identity
`ϖ_2 ∘ ⟨d̃⟩_{r+1} = ⟨d̃ mod⟩_r ∘ ϖ_2`) for the diamonds, directly.  Only the `U'_p` clause then
needs 2.3.  **All four clauses are verified numerically in `04` (checks `(E2)`, `(E2d)`), for five
`(N_g,p,r)` and two primes.**

## §3. Step 3 — well-definedness of `π_r` (the real content)

Let `A_s := Z_p[ X_ℓ, X_{U_p}, X_{U_ℓ}, Y_d : d ∈ (Z/N_gp^s)^× ]` and
`ev_s : A_s → End_{Z_p}(S_s)` the evaluation of `01` §1, so `𝕋'_s = im(ev_s)` (the image is already
a finite `Z_p`-module, hence closed).  Let `φ_r : A_{r+1} → A_r` be `X_• ↦ X_•`,
`Y_{d̃} ↦ Y_{d̃ mod N_gp^r}`.

**Theorem 3.1.**  `ker(ev_{r+1}) ⊆ ker(ev_r ∘ φ_r)`.  Hence `φ_r` descends to a `Z_p`-algebra
homomorphism `π_r : 𝕋'_{r+1} → 𝕋'_r` with `π_r(T'_ℓ) = T'_ℓ`, `π_r(U'_p) = U'_p`,
`π_r(U'_ℓ) = U'_ℓ`, `π_r(⟨d̃⟩) = ⟨d̃ mod N_gp^r⟩`.

*Proof.*  By Proposition 2.4, for every generator `g` of `A_{r+1}`,
`ev_{r+1}(g) ∘ ϖ_2^* = ϖ_2^* ∘ ev_r(φ_r g)`.  Both `P ↦ ev_{r+1}(P)∘ϖ_2^*` and
`P ↦ ϖ_2^*∘ev_r(φ_rP)` are `Z_p`-linear in `P`, and for products
`ev_{r+1}(PQ)ϖ_2^* = ev_{r+1}(P)ev_{r+1}(Q)ϖ_2^* = ev_{r+1}(P)ϖ_2^*ev_r(φ_rQ)
= ϖ_2^*ev_r(φ_rP)ev_r(φ_rQ) = ϖ_2^*ev_r(φ_r(PQ))`.  So by induction on monomials,

    ev_{r+1}(P) ∘ ϖ_2^* = ϖ_2^* ∘ ev_r(φ_r P)      for all P ∈ A_{r+1}.        (★)

If `ev_{r+1}(P) = 0` then `ϖ_2^* ∘ ev_r(φ_rP) = 0`, and `ϖ_2^*` is **injective** (2.4), so
`ev_r(φ_rP) = 0`.  ∎

> **This is the step that (G1)–(G4) alone cannot supply.**  `ϖ_{1,*}` is not injective, so the
> identity `ϖ_{1,*}ev_{r+1}(P) = ev_r(φ_rP)ϖ_{1,*}` gives nothing when `ev_{r+1}(P) = 0`.  The
> inclusion of kernels runs *against* the direction of `ϖ_{1,*}`, and is supplied by the injection
> `ϖ_2^*` running the other way.  Hida's printed tower (`02` §4) supplies the analogous step for the
> **unprimed** algebra with the injection `ϖ_1^* : J_r ↪ J_s` (Picard functoriality); Theorem 3.1 is
> its primed counterpart, and the numerics in `04` show the two injections are genuinely different
> (`(E1)` for `U'_p` FAILS, `(E2)` for `U^t_p` FAILS).

**Corollary 3.2 (the intertwining, for all `t`).**  For every `t ∈ 𝕋'_{r+1}`,
`ϖ_{1,*} ∘ t = π_r(t) ∘ ϖ_{1,*}` in `Hom_{Z_p}(S_{r+1}, S_r)`.

*Proof.*  Exactly the induction of (★) with `ϖ_{1,*}` on the other side: the base cases are
(G1)–(G4) of Step 1, `Z_p`-linearity is clear, and for products
`ϖ_{1,*}(tu) = π_r(t)ϖ_{1,*}u = π_r(t)π_r(u)ϖ_{1,*} = π_r(tu)ϖ_{1,*}`.  Every `t ∈ 𝕋'_{r+1}` is
`ev_{r+1}(P)` for some `P`, and `π_r(t) = ev_r(φ_rP)` is well defined by 3.1.  ∎

**Corollary 3.3 (uniqueness).**  `π_r(t)` is the unique element of `𝕋'_r` (indeed of
`End_{Z_p}(S_r)`) with `ϖ_{1,*}t = π_r(t)ϖ_{1,*}`.

*Proof.*  If `s ϖ_{1,*} = 0` then `s ϖ_{1,*}ϖ_1^* = s·p^2 = 0` on `S_r`, and `S_r` is torsion-free,
so `s = 0`.  ∎  *(Sharper, on ordinary parts: `e'ϖ_{1,*}` is **onto** `e'S_r`, Ohta99 Lemma (1.2.12),
p.249 — `02` §3 — so `s` is determined outright.  See `04` §3 for the numerical confirmation.)*

## §4. Step 4 — surjectivity

**Theorem 4.1.**  `π_r : 𝕋'_{r+1} → 𝕋'_r` is surjective.

*Proof.*  `𝕋'_r` is generated as a `Z_p`-algebra by `T'_ℓ (ℓ∤N_gp)`, `U'_p`, `U'_ℓ (ℓ|N_g)` and
`⟨d⟩ (d ∈ (Z/N_gp^r)^×)`.  The first three are `π_r`-images of the like-named generators of
`𝕋'_{r+1}`.  For `⟨d⟩`: reduction `(Z/N_gp^{r+1})^× ↠ (Z/N_gp^r)^×` is surjective, so
`⟨d⟩ = π_r(⟨d̃⟩)` for any lift `d̃`.  The image of a `Z_p`-algebra map is a `Z_p`-subalgebra, hence
contains the subalgebra generated by these, which is `𝕋'_r`.  ∎

*(No completion subtlety: `𝕋'_r` is the honest subalgebra generated by the operators, and it is
automatically a finite `Z_p`-module, hence closed; no closure step is needed.  Note `π_r` is
**never injective** for `r ≥ 1` — see the ranks in `04` §4.)*

## §5. Step 5 — descent to the ordinary parts

**Theorem 5.1.**  `π_r(e'_{r+1}) = e'_r`, so `π_r` restricts to a surjective ring homomorphism
`R_{r+1} = e'_ord𝕋'_{r+1} ↠ e'_ord𝕋'_r = R_r`, and Corollaries 3.2, 3.3 hold verbatim on the
ordinary parts.

*Proof.*  `𝕋'_s` is a finite `Z_p`-module, so any `Z_p`-linear map `𝕋'_{r+1} → 𝕋'_r` is continuous
for the `p`-adic topologies.  `e'_{r+1} = lim_n (U'_p)^{n!}` converges in `𝕋'_{r+1}`; applying `π_r`,
`π_r(e'_{r+1}) = lim_n π_r(U'_p)^{n!} = lim_n (U'_p)^{n!} = e'_r`.  Since `e'` is a central
idempotent, `e'𝕋'_s` is the quotient ring `𝕋'_s/(1−e')`, and `π_r` carries `(1−e'_{r+1})` into
`(1−e'_r)`; surjectivity is inherited.  ∎

## §6. Statement proved

> **Theorem (H_pi).**  Let `p` be odd, `p ∤ N_g`, `r ≥ 1`.  There is a **unique** homomorphism of
> `Z_p`-algebras `π_r : 𝕋'_{N_gp^{r+1}} → 𝕋'_{N_gp^r}` satisfying
> `ϖ_{1,*} ∘ t = π_r(t) ∘ ϖ_{1,*}` in `Hom_{Z_p}(S_{r+1}, S_r)` for all `t`.  It is given on
> generators by `T'_ℓ ↦ T'_ℓ`, `U'_p ↦ U'_p`, `U'_ℓ ↦ U'_ℓ`, `⟨d̃⟩ ↦ ⟨d̃ mod N_gp^r⟩`; it is
> **surjective**; it satisfies `π_r(e'_ord) = e'_ord` and hence induces a surjective ring
> homomorphism `π_r : R_{r+1} ↠ R_r` with the same intertwining on `e'_ord`-parts.  Everything is
> integral over `Z_p`; no ordinarity hypothesis is used; the same statement holds for either carrier
> `H^1_ét(X̄_•,Z_p(1))` or `H^1_ét(Ȳ_•,Z_p(1))`.

## §8. Failure modes checked (why this is not a circular or vacuous proof)

1. **Is `ϖ_2^*` really different from `ϖ_1^*` here?**  Yes.  `04` verifies that `ϖ_1^*` does **not**
   commute with `U'_p` (check `(E1)` for `U'_p` FAILS in all 10 runs), while `ϖ_2^*` does.  If the
   two agreed, Hida's printed tower would already be H_pi.
2. **Is `ϖ_{1,*}` really the primed side?**  Yes.  `04` verifies `ϖ_{1,*}` does **not** commute with
   the unprimed `U^t_p` (check `(G)` for `U^t_p` FAILS in all 10 runs).
3. **Could `π_r` fail to exist because the level-`r` relations are strictly more numerous?**  That is
   the direction (WD) needs and Theorem 3.1 supplies; `04` §4 confirms it on the nose by computing
   both relation lattices for small `(N_g,p,r)` and checking the containment, and by checking that
   the *reverse* containment fails (so `π_r` is onto and not injective — no accidental isomorphism).
4. **Torsion.**  Every division performed is by a unit: `p^2` is only ever *cancelled against
   torsion-freeness*, never inverted, and `(U'_p)^{-1}` exists integrally on `e'`-parts.
