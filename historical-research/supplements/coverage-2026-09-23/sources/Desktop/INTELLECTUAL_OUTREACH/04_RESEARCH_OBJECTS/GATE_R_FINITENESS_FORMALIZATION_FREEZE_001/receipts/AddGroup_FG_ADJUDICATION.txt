`AddGroup.FG` IN BOTH RETURNS — ADJUDICATED, AND A DEFECT IN MY OWN MANDATE AND SCANNER

WHAT HAPPENED.  `TASK.md` listed `AddGroup.FG` among things that may not be used.  Both returns
contain the name.  I checked before judging.

FINDING: NO VIOLATION.  In each return `AddGroup.FG` occurs exactly once, in the form

    haveI : AddGroup.FG ((P01.Ecurve ε m q).toAffine.Point) :=
      AddGroup.fg_iff.mpr ⟨…, hgen, hfin⟩            (run A, line 131)
      AddGroup.fg_iff.mpr ⟨S, hStop, hSfin⟩          (run B, line 140)

i.e. **derived with a proof term, from a finite generating set the run itself constructed.**  A
binder-position test found ZERO occurrences in a hypothesis position in either return, and the
type-identity check confirms the shipped signature is unchanged: the theorem carries exactly
`Params`, `H41` and the prime instance.  Finite generation is an internal intermediate, not a
premise, and the result is not conditional on it.  Both then conclude via
`AddCommGroup.finite_of_fg_torsion` with torsion supplied by the frozen `GateR.every_point_torsion`.

MY MANDATE WAS AMBIGUOUS.  The clause reads "Premises that would make the result conditional.
Mordell–Weil finite generation, `Module.Finite ℤ`, `Module.Free ℤ`, `AddGroup.FG`, or any assumed
finiteness … hypothesis".  The governing words are "premises" and "assumed", and both runs read it
that way, which is the reading I intended.  But the clause sits under a heading that says "What may
not be used", and a reader could have taken the name to be banned outright — which would have
forced a contorted proof or a false failure report.  The defect is mine.  A prohibition on a
*premise* must say so in the same sentence as the name, and should name the test: not in a binder.

MY SCANNER COULD NOT HAVE CAUGHT A REAL VIOLATION.  When I rewrote the construct scanner after the
axiom defect, I dropped `AddGroup.FG`, `Module.Finite`, `Module.Free`, `Fintype` and `Mordell` from
its pattern list.  So the corrected scanner tests the construct prohibitions and NOT the premise
prohibitions.  I found the FG occurrence through the divergence measurement, not through the scan.

This is the same defect family as findings 1 and 5 of the Ultra audit — a check narrower than its
stated scope — appearing for the third time, but for the first time found by me rather than by an
external reviewer.  Recorded, not repaired: the adjudication above is the correction, and the
binder-position test is now the check of record for premise prohibitions.

MATHEMATICAL NOTE, offered as an observation.  The route both runs chose is arguably better than the
one I had privately verified: a finitely generated torsion abelian group is finite, and finite
generation follows from a finite generating set produced by strict height descent.  It uses the
already-certified `bounded_halving` and `translateMax` rather than re-deriving an orbit bound.
