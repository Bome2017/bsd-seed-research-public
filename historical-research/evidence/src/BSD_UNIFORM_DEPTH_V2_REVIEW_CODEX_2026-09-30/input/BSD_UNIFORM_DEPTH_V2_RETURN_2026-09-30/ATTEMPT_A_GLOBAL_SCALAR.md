    4*c_infinity*Tam/#tors^2 = 4*2*16/4 = 32.

Thus n_3=288 is a well-defined p-independent integer computed from permitted finite arithmetic. The brief records matching residues at its listed primes. Defining this integer is not prohibited by K-F.

    C2(p,ell) = (a_ell-2)*n_3*R_(p,ell) mod p

for all p outside a fixed finite set is not obtained from the 3-primary computation. The paired states X_0 and X_1 of Attempt (iii) at another prime have identical 3-primary data and different desired vanishing outcomes. The omitted new arrow is a cross-prime specialization theorem, not the arithmetic multiplication 32*9.

**DERIVED — first failing test for the automatic endpoint inference.** K-S. Known local factors and known 3-primary data alone do not distinguish excess primary Selmer at another prime. No K-F prohibition is imposed on the certified input. The independent identity itself remains an unproved conjecture rather than a tested construction.

## Candidate A-3: a common quadratic-moment presentation

**DERIVED — stopping point.** A finite description of the modular-symbol module does not automatically produce a finite integral presentation of the quadratic Kato-moment obstruction with the required base-change law. In particular, a fixed finite list of conductor primes can support order-p characters only when p divides one of the corresponding q-1. Infinitely many p-dependent conductors cannot be replaced by that fixed list without a new theorem controlling their quadratic moments.

**DERIVED — first failing test.** A proved nonzero determinant with the required Selmer/moment comparison would discriminate the paired states, so that conditional endpoint passes K-S. The construction first fails K-P: the needed uniform reduction from the p-dependent admissible conductors to a proved common finite integral presentation is missing. The material presented does not establish that this reduction is impossible.
