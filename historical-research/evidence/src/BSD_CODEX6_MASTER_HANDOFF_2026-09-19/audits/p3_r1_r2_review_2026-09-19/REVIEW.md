### 1.2 Local factor derivation

The retained input is split multiplicative reduction at \(q\), so \(a_q(f)=1\). Inert base change has residue norm \(q^2\), and Frobenius on the one-dimensional inertia-invariant Steinberg space has eigenvalue \(a_q(f)^2=1\). Therefore

\[
L_w(f/K,\chi,1)=\bigl(1-a_q(f)^2\chi(w)q^{-2}\bigr)^{-1}
=\bigl(1-q^{-2}\bigr)^{-1}.
\]

Removing this Euler factor multiplies the primitive value by

\[
\mathcal E_q=1-q^{-2}=\frac{(q-1)(q+1)}{q^2}.
\]

The same expression follows from base-change factorization: split multiplicative \(E\) contributes \((1-q^{-1})^{-1}\), while its inert quadratic twist contributes \((1+q^{-1})^{-1}\).

Here \(q-1=2242\), \(q+1=2244=3\cdot748\), and \(3\nmid2242\cdot748\cdot2243\). Hence

\[
\boxed{v_3(\mathcal E_{2243})=1.}
\]

This factor is constant in the anticyclotomic variable. Its μ is therefore positive: one with valuation normalized by \(v_3(3)=1\), or the positive ramification-index multiple if measured by a uniformizer of an enlarged coefficient DVR. It is never zero.

Other removed factors away from 3 are integral: their denominators are powers of primes other than 3, and their Hecke and character values are integral. They cannot cancel this valuation. Under the audited interpolation comparison, CH's primitive μ-zero result and the passed period-unit comparison thus give an imprimitive series with μ **at least one**, rather than zero. This elementary local calculation is not a replay of an elliptic-curve arithmetic certificate.

### 1.3 Nonzero restriction is insufficient for the proof being invoked

[SU], Proposition 12.3.6 and proof, **p. 202**, assert exclusion of **every** extended height-one prime \(QI[[\Gamma_K]]\), for \(Q\subset I[[\Gamma_K^+]]\). The proof writes

\[
L^\Sigma_{\boldsymbol f,K,\xi}=\sum_{i\ge0}a_i(\gamma_- -1)^i,
\qquad a_i\in I[[\Gamma_K^+]],
\]

specializes at weight two and \(\gamma_+=1\), and explicitly uses μ zero to obtain some \(\phi(a_i)\in A^\times\). This unit coefficient, rather than mere nonzero restriction, is its reason that the coefficients cannot all belong to an extended prime.

A nonzero series with a fixed factor of 3 does not supply such a coefficient. Replacing μ zero by nonvanishing therefore does not justify the target's use of the printed proof. Conversely, positive μ at this one specialization does **not** by itself prove that the full family lies in an extended height-one prime; this review makes no such assertion. Removing a fixed factor on both analytic and algebraic sides would require new identities and an argument that the altered support statement suffices. Neither is supplied in §3.6, and neither has been constructed here.
