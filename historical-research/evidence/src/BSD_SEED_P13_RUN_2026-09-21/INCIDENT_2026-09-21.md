# Incident: first launch wrote through reuse symlinks into the sealed corpus

**Outcome: no damage. Full corpus re-verified, 2333/2333 clean. Recorded because the hazard was real.**

## What I did wrong

`preflight.py` symlinked the 2333 reused segments into `run/ap/` and `run/logs/` — copying the p11 pattern — but did
**not** create the corresponding `run/seg/<s>.done` markers. `run_p3.sh`'s worker script gates solely on those markers:

    s=$1; [ -f $OUT/seg/$s.done ] && exit 0

With no markers, stage W treated all 3281 segments as new. `wverify` writes its coefficient output to `$OUT/ap/...`
and its log to `$OUT/logs/ver_$s.log` — both of which were **symlinks into sealed certificate inputs**, so the writes
followed the links and overwrote the originals.

Confirmed from mtimes: `BSD_SEED_2243_12_P3_RIGOROUS_RUN_2026-09-19/run/ap/ap_000000.bin` and `.../logs/ver_0.log`
were both rewritten at 13:31:37, after the 13:30:57 launch.

## Why it was harmless, and why that is luck rather than design

The coefficient producer is deterministic, so the rewritten bytes were identical to the sealed bytes. A full re-hash of
all 2333 segments against `CERTIFICATE_BINDINGS.json` — both coefficient files and verifier records — returned
**0 damaged** (`POST_ABORT_INTEGRITY.json`, 17.8 s).

Two ways this could have gone badly instead:
1. I killed the workers with `pkill -9` while they were mid-segment. A signal landing during a write to a sealed file
   would have truncated it.
2. Any nondeterminism in the producer would have silently replaced sealed bytes with different ones.

Neither happened. Both were live during the ~2 minutes the first launch ran.

## Fix applied

Created `run/seg/<s>.done` for `s = 0..2332` **before** relaunching, so the worker exits on those segments without
invoking `apwit` or `wverify` at all. Verified: 0 reused segments lack a marker, 948 remain to generate — exactly the
gate's figure. Post-relaunch, workers are on segments 2333+ and the sealed file's mtime is unchanged from the abort.

Spurious witness files and producer logs from the aborted attempt were deleted.

## Rule for any future reuse-by-symlink

Symlinking inputs into a pipeline that writes to the same paths is unsafe on its own. The skip condition the pipeline
actually tests must be satisfied **before** launch — here, the done markers, not the presence of the files. Better
still, reuse should be read-only by construction; symlinks are not.
