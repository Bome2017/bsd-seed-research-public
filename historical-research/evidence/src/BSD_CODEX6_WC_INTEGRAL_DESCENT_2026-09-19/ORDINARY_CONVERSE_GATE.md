# Ordinary-prime equivalence gate

Status: proved deductions from the primary-source statements identified below, relative to the retained seed hypotheses. No Lean certification or mathematical-novelty claim. The finite-depth converse has a narrower verification status than the primitive converse.

Let P be the primes outside S0={2,3,5,2243,4547,5843}, and let O be the good ordinary primes in P. Keep h_p and Ota admissibility exactly as in EXACT_WC_ANTECEDENT.md.

## Theorem O1: the primitive converse

For every p in O,

    h_p=0  iff  Sha(E/Q)[p^infinity]=0.

The forward direction is Ota 6.4 as used in WC. Here is the converse, including the auxiliary-prime and normalization checks.

1. Castella-Sano, arXiv:2601.14504v1, Theorem B, proves M_infinity(delta)=ord_p(Tam_E). Its assumptions are p>3, residual surjectivity, a modular parametrization with p-unit Manin constant, and good ordinary reduction. For this seed the retained all-odd residual theorem supplies surjectivity, the retained parametrization has Manin factor a power of 2, and Tam_E=16. Thus M_infinity(delta)=0. This theorem asserts that some Kurihara number is a p-unit; it does not assert that its conductor has two prime factors.
2. Kim, arXiv:2203.12159v6, Theorem 1.10, applies: p>=5, residual surjectivity, p-unit Manin constant, E(Q_p)[p]=0, and p-unit Tamagawa factors. Non-anomalous good reduction implies E(Q_p)[p]=0. For the seed at p>=7, rational 2-torsion and Hasse imply p does not divide #E(F_p): a positive even multiple of p would be at least 2p>p+1+2sqrt(p).
3. Assume Sha[p^infinity]=0. Select a mod-p nonzero Kurihara number delta_n with the minimum number of prime factors. Kim's last assertion gives nu(n)=rank E(Q)=2. His localization isomorphism gives

       Sel_p(E/Q) ≅ direct_sum_{ell|n} E(F_ell)/p E(F_ell).

   The left side has dimension two, by the Kummer exact sequence and Sha[p]=0. Every one of the two summands on the right has dimension at least one, since the Kurihara prime conditions imply p divides #E(F_ell). Hence each has dimension exactly one. Therefore E(F_ell)[p] is cyclic for both primes. This resolves the fact that Kim's and Castella-Sano's displayed prime sets impose trace/determinant congruences, without explicitly imposing Ota's cyclicity condition.
4. At n=ell_1 ell_2, the mixed degree-two coefficient of the projection of Ota's theta_n to the maximal p-quotient is the sum of the plus modular symbols times the two discrete logarithms. The minus terms cancel under a -> -a. Changing primitive roots multiplies this coefficient by a p-unit. Ota's period is the positive generator of real projections of the period lattice; Kim and Castella-Sano use the integral over E(R). The latter is twice the former, so Ota's plus symbol is twice theirs. In particular the normalization ratio is a p-unit. Thus delta_n nonzero mod p gives a nonzero mixed coefficient modulo p.
5. Both auxiliary primes are now Ota-admissible. Ota 5.17 places theta_n in I^2, and the degree-two class has depth zero. Therefore h_p=0.

No FIN assumption was used to define any symbol or source. The converse proof does assume the specific primary vanishing whose equivalence is being proved. It is not a proof of that vanishing.

## What the theorem says about the uniform tail

For an arbitrary finite set F of ordinary primes,

    h_p=0 for all p in O minus F
      iff
    Sha[p^infinity]=0 for all p in O minus F.

Consequently, finite support of positive/infinite depths on the ordinary tail is exactly finite support of the ordinary primary Sha groups. A uniform bounded-content antecedent does include cross-prime information beyond the statement that each primary group is finite: it includes eventual vanishing. But this is precisely the finite-support part of FIN, expressed in Ota coordinates; it is not an independently weaker uniform structure.

For example, the abstract torsion group direct_sum_p (Z/p)^2 has every primary group finite and infinitely many nonzero primary groups. This is an algebraic counterexample to the inference from pointwise finiteness to finite support, not a proposed Sha group for this curve.

WC also needs h_p finite at the finitely many remaining primes. Theorem O1 alone does not settle those finite-depth conditions. Do not turn an ordinary-tail equivalence into an unqualified theorem J_E!=0 iff global FIN.

## Finite depth: the exact checked boundary

For every p in P, Ota 5.17 gives

    h_p<infinity  =>  Sha[p^infinity] finite.

For p in O, Kim 1.6 and 1.8 give a converse using the collection of Kurihara numbers as printed: if Sha[p^infinity] is finite, ord(delta)=2 and a two-prime Kurihara number is nonzero in its finite quotient. With Castella-Sano, the minimum valuation of two-prime Kurihara numbers is length_Zp Sha[p^infinity]. These numbers use reduction modulo the ideal generated by ell-1 and a_ell-ell-1 for all auxiliary primes, so nonzero reduction would give a nonzero mixed Ota coefficient at a larger modulus IF the primes satisfy Ota cyclicity.

At positive depth, the dimension-two argument above no longer forces cyclicity: Sel_p can have dimension greater than two. The printed prime set includes residual Frobenius equal to the identity. Kim's Kolyvagin-system discussion separately invokes the cyclic local quotient condition (Theorem 2.1), but a fully audited prime-replacement argument preserving the relevant valuation and two-prime support was not supplied in this session. Therefore this report does NOT assert

    Sha[p^infinity] finite => h_p<infinity

for the exact Ota-admissible set at positive depth, nor the stronger estimate h_p<=length Sha. The corresponding unrestricted Kurihara statements ARE supplied by Kim's printed theorem. This is a transfer qualification, not a claimed disproof or a finding of error in that paper.
