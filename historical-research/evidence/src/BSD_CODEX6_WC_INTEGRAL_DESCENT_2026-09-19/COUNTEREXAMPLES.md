# Counterexamples and scope controls

These are exact algebraic countermodels. None is a claimed computation of this curve's Sha or modular symbols.

## C1. Even a surjective whole map does not control the distinguished element

Let M=Z_p^2, z=(0,1), and phi:M->Z/p^k send (a,b) to a modulo p^k. The source element z is primitive, phi is surjective, and coker(phi)=0, killed by 1. Nevertheless phi(z)=0. Thus a bound on the cokernel of the whole specialization map is insufficient. UC evaluates z_E first and controls the resulting rank-one image ideal, which avoids this logical defect.

## C2. Fixed source denominators do not bound image content

The fixed source Z with z=1 maps into Z/p^3 by multiplication by p^2 at each p. Its nonzero image has depth two at every p. No nonzero integer has valuation at least two at all primes. The source and all maps are integral; their images still have infinitely many prime defects.

## C3. Truncated zero is not a finite-depth witness

The integer p^k represents zero in Z/p^k. Its depth there is infinity. Recording the integer lift's valuation k, or forming the preimage ideal generated by a zero coefficient together with p^k and reading its generator as witness content, manufactures a false witness. UC separately requires a nonzero evaluated ideal.

## C4. Primitive local data can have zero degree-two jet

In Z_p[C_p], for p>=5, X^3=(sigma-1)^3 has a unit group-ring coefficient and is primitive as a Z_p-vector, but lies in I^3. Its image in I^2/I^3 is zero. Even an index-one integral regulator cannot turn this into a nonzero quadratic jet. The actual global Kato class must be controlled in the augmentation filtration, not just in its underlying lattice.

## C5. Pointwise finite defects do not give finite support

The torsion group direct_sum_{p>=7}(Z/p)^2 has finite nonzero primary groups at infinitely many p. Likewise the finite free complex over product_p Z_p with differential (p^2)_p is rationally acyclic at each p and has finite local cokernels, but has no nonzero common integer annihilator. The direct-sum example already has square-order local groups with nondegenerate alternating pairings. To require those pairings also in the complex example, use the differential diag(p,p) on (product_p Z_p)^2; each local cokernel is then (Z/p)^2 and the same infinite-support obstruction persists.

## C6. Hecke closure cannot repair the evaluated ideal

Let W be generated by the weighted cycles. For every integral Hecke operator T, z_E(TC)=a_T z_E(C), with a_T integral. The image ideal of the Hecke span of W equals z_E(W) when the identity is included. Enlarging to a saturated lattice by dividing by p is a different operation and no longer gives the displayed arithmetic cycles without proof.

## C7. A finite list of tame levels cannot cover the unbounded prime set

For finite G, #G kills every positive augmentation graded quotient. If p does not divide #G, the quotient over Z_p vanishes. For finitely many tame levels, only finitely many primes divide their group orders. Those levels cannot supply nonzero WC witnesses at the remaining primes. This is a finite-stage statement; it does not prohibit a completed or derived construction.

## C8. An unbounded integer is not a computable prime cutoff

Knowing abstractly that some A belongs to J_E is sufficient for the theorem WC, but does not provide a practical list of exceptional primes. Conversely, finding witnesses at a finite list of p gives no value of A that controls all remaining p. No executable global proof follows from a finite successful sample.
