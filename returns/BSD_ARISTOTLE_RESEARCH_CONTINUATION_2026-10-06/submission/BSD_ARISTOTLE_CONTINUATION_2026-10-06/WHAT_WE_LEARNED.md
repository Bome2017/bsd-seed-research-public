## Mathematical starting point

BFR changes the domain of the forward comparison. It does not constrain the
size of the receiver. Its accepted facts can now be reused without redoing
the local calculations, including the repair at 4547. The written proof still
imports residual surjectivity, basis saturation, Tamagawa data and parity.

The independent review also repaired a collision test. Unequal j-invariants
alone did not prove E2[p] differs from E[p]. Frobenius traces at 11 and 19 now
prove that nonisomorphism for every odd p. This excludes a direct equivariant
linear transfer from the other elliptic factor. It does not exclude every
correspondence or higher construction involving the genus-two curve, whose
Jacobian also contains E. Its splitting by itself gives no new bound.

## What changes in the successor task

The earlier prompt already permitted discovery and demanded a seed implication.
The first run nonetheless screened old mechanisms and finished with a local
extension. We cannot determine from that alone whether the main limitation was
task selection, available time, or mathematical difficulty. A stronger prompt
does not by itself solve that difficulty.

This successor makes three concrete changes:

1. It supplies the accepted return as a starting lemma, with corrections, so
   the system can spend its work on the next unknown arithmetic operation.
2. It separates construction from formalization: first develop and test the
   proposed control mechanism, then formalize the new steps that matter to it.
3. It requires a comparison of what the old facts allowed and what the new
   theorem actually constrains. Another correct local fact can be retained,
   but cannot silently replace work on the mechanism.

Useful partial progress is allowed. The task is not “prove BSD immediately or
fail,” and the wall atlas is not a blacklist of mathematical families.
