## 2. Finite-group descent

The central involution (-I,+1) kills the cohomology of G0 with coefficients V
or P. Thus Gamma splits as P semidirect G0. It also annihilates every
equivariant map from exterior-square P to V.

The key remaining group is H1(G0,Hom(P,V)). The twisted summand vanishes by
the central C2 action. For each End(V) summand, restrict to the cyclic Sylow
subgroup generated by u=I+E12. The norm on End(V) is zero for p≥5: Ad(u^i)
is quadratic in i, and the sums of 1,i,i² vanish modulo p. The H1 quotient
has basis I and E21. Diag(t,1) acts with weights t^-1 and t^-2, respectively,
including the action on the cocycle argument. Neither is the trivial
character for p≥5. Sylow restriction is injective, so H1(G0,Hom(P,V))=0.
This argument includes p=5 and does not use Maschke's theorem for G0.

The degree-two Hochschild–Serre filtration therefore embeds H2(Gamma,V) in
H2(P,V)^G0. A central extension of P by V has a commutator map from
exterior-square P; invariance kills this map. The remaining invariant classes
are abelian extensions, classified by the p-th-power map P→V. Their space is
Hom_G0(P,V)=F_p².

Suppose an extension of Gamma by V realizes a nonzero such map phi. Lift the
chosen G0 complement using H2(G0,V)=0. The preimage X of P is then an abelian
group with G0 action and pX=V. Since P is the particular semisimple module
V²⊕U, choose a G0-submodule V0 of P on which phi is an isomorphism. Its preimage
Y in X is free of rank two over Z/p², and Y/pY identifies with the standard
V. The G0 action would lift the standard representation to GL2(Z/p²).

This is impossible: a lift x of the order-p transvection I+E12 satisfies

    x^p = I+p E12 != I mod p².

To see this directly, write x=J(I+pC). Modulo p² its power is
J^p times I+p times a sum of conjugates J^-i C J^i. The sum vanishes modulo p
by the same quadratic-in-i calculation. This identity and the resulting
no-section statement are freshly compiled in NoLift.lean.

Consequently H2(Gamma,V)=0. This proof already appears for U=V epsilon in
the retained RECEIVER_DESCENT.md; allowing U=0 does not change it.

## 5. The coefficient place

First take good p or p in {2243,5843}. F_p splits at p, so K'_w=Q_p(E[p]).
The local Kummer quotient has dimension one and is generated by the primitive
formal point R_p. Consequently the local division classes of P1,P2,R_p are
multiples of one class; adjoining Q3 already supplies the full completion L_w
over Q_p(E[p]).

Write G for the image on V. For ordinary good or multiplicative reduction,
V has upper-triangular characters chi1,chi2, with inertia characters omega,1.
If p does not divide |G|, H1(G,V)=0 by averaging. Otherwise the upper-unipotent
Sylow subgroup is normal and the quotient has order prime to p. Its H1
quotient has character chi2²/chi1, nontrivial on inertia (omega^-1). Hence
H1(G,V)=0 in this case as well.

The local translation subgroup is nonzero and contained in the formal
chi1-line. A division point can be chosen in the formal group; all its
Galois differences are formal p-torsion. The two inertia characters are
distinct, so Hom_G(chi1,V) has dimension one. Inflation–restriction now bounds
the kernel of H1(Q_p,V)→H1(L_w,V) by one. It contains the finite Kummer line,
so equals it.

For supersingular reduction over Q_p, use the retained full nonsplit-Cartan
normalizer description. Its order is prime to p, V is irreducible, and
End_G(V)=F_p: the Cartan centralizer F_(p²) is cut down to F_p by Frobenius.
The nonzero local translation subgroup is V. The same inflation–restriction
bound again makes the local kernel exactly the finite line. The Frobenius
step is essential.

For p=4547, retain BFR and E(Q_p)[p]=0. Local duality gives H1 dimension two,
while the Kummer condition has dimension one. Its containing pulled-back
condition therefore has excess at most one. No exact local calculation at
4547 is needed or claimed here.

## 6. Remove the exceptional global relaxation

For p=19,59,487, all conditions except the single q listed in (2) are finite.
The submitted program and separate nodal-torus calculation show that each of
P1 and P2 has a nonzero image in f_q. One point suffices. Let k be its global
Kummer class, and let c belong to S_p.

At every place other than q, both localizations are finite, so their local
Tate pairing is zero. Global reciprocity forces the remaining pairing

    <loc_q c, loc_q k>_q = 0.

Because loc_q k spans f_q and the finite Kummer line is its own annihilator,
loc_q c belongs to f_q. Thus c is in Sel_p. This proves S_p=Sel_p without
requiring that L_q itself equal f_q.

Equivalently, Greenberg–Wiles for the relaxation gives the added global
dimension 1-eta_p when L_q is full, and eta_p=1 from the point computation.
Both arguments agree; the reciprocity proof also covers L_q=f_q at once.
