## 1. Actual adelic calculation for the packet's coarse level

Let B/Q be the indefinite quaternion algebra of the packet, and let K_0=prod_v O_v^times be its coarse Eichler compact open. Thus K_0 contains every scalar finite integral unit, including at primes dividing the Eichler level. Let ell=ell_2 be split in B and prime to the level. Write

    Sh_(K_0)(C)=B^times(Q) \ (H^± x B^times(A_f)) / K_0.

For a in Q_ell^times let i_ell(a) be the central finite adele with component a at ell and component 1 elsewhere. Write a=ell^n u with u in Z_ell^times. Set b=ell^n in Q^times, and define the central finite unit adele k by

    k_ell=u,       k_v=ell^(-n) for finite v!=ell.

Then i_ell(a)=b k. Every k_v is a scalar unit in O_v, so k belongs to K_0. The rational scalar b acts trivially on H^±. Centrality therefore gives the exact double-coset identity

    [z, g i_ell(a)]_(K_0)
      =[z, b g k]_(K_0)
      =[z, g]_(K_0).                                  (MB1)

Consequently **the entire local center Q_ell^times acts trivially on the coarse Shimura curve**, not merely Z_ell^times. This calculation holds on all connected components and does not require a square root, a p-unit comparison, or a choice of Frobenius sign.

## 2. The fixed fine-level forgetful map kills the same action

Let K_1 be the compact open obtained by imposing Gamma_1(5) structure on the same coarse level, and retain the packet's connected/projective convention for the common central sign. There is the geometric forgetful map

    pi: Sh_(K_1) -> Sh_(K_0),     [z,g]_(K_1) |-> [z,g]_(K_0).

It satisfies

    pi circ r(i_ell(a)) = pi                            (MB2)

by (MB1). Here r denotes right translation. Upstairs the action need not be trivial: after removing b as above, the only potentially nontrivial residual scalar level action is k_5=ell^(-n) modulo 5. In the projective sign convention it factors through F_5^times/{+1,−1}. This explicitly locates the possible diamond twist rather than silently identifying the fine models.

All away-level Hecke double-coset correspondences commute with this central action. The forgetful morphism intertwines these correspondences by its definition. Thus no additional Hecke scalar is generated by the cancellation (MB2).

The degree remains the geometric index 12 from the fixed-cover note. Descent of a geometric map does not change that degree.

## 3. Exact descent implication

Here is the precise sufficient source statement.

**CD.** After a common geometric identification with the standard fine Shimura reduction, the Galois descent datum defining Ihara's ell-canonical model differs from the ordinary descent datum by right translations through central elements i_ell(a_sigma), for sigma in the relevant decomposition group. The coarse and fine constructions use the functorial level-forgetting map, and their special sets correspond to the supersingular sets of the packet.

Under CD, (MB2) makes pi compatible with the ordinary coarse descent datum: for every sigma, composing the modified upstairs descent with pi gives the same morphism as composing ordinary descent with pi. Galois descent of morphisms then supplies

    pi_Ih: Y_Ih -> X_(F_(ell^2)).                       (MB3)

The twist is invisible downstairs by (MB1). In particular the coarse Ihara model is the standard coarse reduction in this comparison. Supersingular sets map to supersingular sets, because the underlying map is the geometric level-forgetting map. Its degree is 12 and its Hecke compatibility is preserved by descent.

This is an actual implication with an explicit formula, not the assertion that a forgetful map should work. It establishes

    CD => MB.

It is insensitive to whether the source writes a_sigma or a_sigma^(-1), or whether its arithmetic/geometric Frobenius convention changes a sign: the entire central subgroup acts trivially downstairs. It is **not** insensitive to an additional noncentral automorphism or a different identification of the geometric models. Those must be excluded by the source definition of CD.

## 4. What was checked in the primary source

Ihara's 1998 survey, Section 4, constructs the ell-canonical model over the stated quadratic unramified extension of its decomposition field. It refers to `Congruence relations and Shimura curves I`, Section 6, for the definition; its summary calls the key operation division by the local scalars. The survey also supplies the functorial finite-field model theorem used in the fixed-cover note.

However the survey does **not** print the adelic compact subgroup or the Galois descent cocycle defining that operation. The summary is consistent with CD, but I did not treat its informal phrase as a substitute for checking the exact datum. In particular it does not, on the inspected page alone, prove that no additional noncentral descent automorphism occurs in the packet's chosen identification.

A bounded primary-source retrieval found Part II in the University of Tokyo repository. I visually inspected its first page, printed page 301. It explicitly says that Section 6, "The case of Shimura curves", is omitted and that Shimura curves do not appear explicitly there. Thus Part II is not a replacement source for the missing Part I Section 6 definition. The full Part II was not read.

Part I Section 6 was not retrieved in this pass. Search absence is not a claim that it is unavailable or that CD fails.

Therefore I retain **CD as a precise source-binding condition**. The adelic cancellation in Sections 1–3 is proved, but the current return does not upgrade MB or C4 to unconditional source-matched status.

## 5. What this changes in the continuation
