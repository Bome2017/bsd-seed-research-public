## The ordinary class source is present despite Cl_S=0

The curve is `[1,0,1,-5,0]`, with points

\[
P_1=(-1,2),\qquad P_2=(3,2).
\]

The saved degree-eight field has signature (2,3), ordinary class group C3, and roots-of-unity order 2. With S={2,3,359}, the S-class group is trivial. The full allowed-valuation source has dimension 13 and is generated by S-units. Its valuation-zero subspace V0 nevertheless has dimension 5. The ordinary unit subspace has dimension 4, and the exact ideal boundary V0→Cl(L)[3] has rank 1 and precisely those units as its kernel.

Thus this example is not an appended Cl_S-lift example. It is a nontrivial **ordinary** class lift inside a suitable combination of S-unit generators. The candidate code correctly recovers the boundary from one-third of the complete ideal valuations. Equating Cl_S=0 with absence of class-supported directions would have missed the experiment.

The independently checked dimensions are:

| Ordinary class 3-torsion | 1 |

The chosen bare class lift has nonzero scalar defect and nonzero local defects at 3 and 359. Its local defect at 2 is zero. But these raw defects do **not** reject its class: allowing ordinary-unit corrections makes the quotient defect zero for the scalar block, scalar-plus-additivity block, local block, and full joint block.

For every reported coupling block I checked the full annihilator dimension, not just that its rows annihilate unit images. Therefore the saved quotient really is the target modulo all allowed unit repairs. I checked the reported repair equations directly.

In the full joint block, the repaired class has the exact source coordinates of

\[
\boxed{P_1+2P_2\quad\text{modulo }3.}
\]

The ordinary class projection of the two point columns is

\[
\boxed{(\pi_0(P_1),\pi_0(P_2))=(1,0)\in\mathbf F_3^2.}
\]

The nonzero ordinary class direction is therefore occupied by an actual point class. The other independent point occupies the admitted unit direction. Rejecting the bare lift because its localization is nonzero would falsely reject a class that becomes a rational-point Kummer class after an allowed unit repair.

## What follows from the saved local computation

The local blocks have image dimensions 1,1,0 at 2,3,359 respectively, and row ranks 3,9,4. The full local-admission kernel already has dimension 2 and contains the two point columns. Thus, subject to the complete-source and actual-local-image inputs, this provides the usual Selmer upper bound of 2. It does not require promoting the six finite split-prime additivity tests into a complete global cohomology test: every true Selmer class lies in the containing local kernel, and the two point classes fill it.

The initially flagged local-precision issue was repaired during this experiment. I independently checked the resulting six saved rational approximants using Python `Fraction` arithmetic: each curve-equation residual F and derivative F' satisfies the exact Hensel inequality, and the resulting error exponent N satisfies `e*N-v(f)-depth >= 0` at every prime above the coefficient place. No agreement of two floating or p-adic runs is substituted for this argument.

A further exact GP replay recomputed each tangent value, its local valuations and residue class in the **original stored local bases**. The verified samples span precisely the saved local images. ## What the experiment teaches about the seed problem

The class-group size and the unit-space size do not alone distinguish excess from points. Here the admitted class quotient is nonzero, but the point boundary surjects onto it. The independently computable feature is **which admitted unit and class directions are already occupied by rational-point evaluations, after the required repair equations are solved**.

This is an actual computed example of that interaction, including the nonzero defect of a bare lift and its repair. It supplies a test case for proposed point/class comparison formulas. It does not prove such a formula for the seed or uniformly in coefficient primes, and does not show that another ordinary class source can always be repaired or filled by points.
