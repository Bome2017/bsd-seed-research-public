## One divisor is enough

Fix the actual optimal quotient `phi:J_0(655)->E`, its differential convention, the identification of supersingular points with the same right-ideal classes used in the saved Brandt computation, and a generator `T` of `E(F_4)/5E(F_4)`.

For a degree-zero supersingular divisor d with `g_saved(d) != 0`, obtain **independently** the reduction `phi(d)` and form

`u_2 = dlog_T(phi(d)) / g_saved(d) mod 5`.

This is the scalar in the prescribed local Kummer normalization, provided all maps and orientations are matched. No full 30-point CM orbit is logically required to fix a one-dimensional eigenline. A known nonzero normalized divisor suffices. Existence of a one-dimensional eigenspace does not compute its comparison with the specified quotient; an arbitrary identification merely chooses the unknown scalar again.

The curve has `a_2=-2`, hence `#E(F_2)=#E(F_4)=5`. In characteristic2 the supersingular j-invariant is0. Choosing its F4 model with Frobenius[-2] makes every odd-order cyclic subgroup Frobenius-stable, so the relevant supersingular X0(655) points are defined overF4. This makes a direct finite-field parametrization a possible alternative to high-degree complex recognition. It does not supply that map algorithmically.

## Admission boundary

Success would be a nonzero finite-field point and an authenticated scalar `u_2`, allowing a normalized control comparison. It would not prove that the scalar is rational or independent of p, it would not turn finite p-adic precision into an exact equality, and it would not settle an unresolved seed prime. The nonunit-scalar experiment in REVIEW.md is cheaper and more discriminating before investing in this normalization.
