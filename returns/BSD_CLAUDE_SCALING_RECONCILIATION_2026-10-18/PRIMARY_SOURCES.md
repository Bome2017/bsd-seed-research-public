# Sources freshly checked

1. Schaefer–Stoll, *How to do a p-descent on an elliptic curve*: https://www.mathe2.uni-bayreuth.de/stoll/papers/p-descent-long.pdf . The actual global cohomology realization includes scalar and incidence-norm constraints; local Selmer conditions are additional. The report does not identify an arbitrary number-field element with a global cohomology class.

2. Biasse–Fieker–Hofmann–Page, *Norm relations and computational problems in number fields*, https://arxiv.org/html/2002.12332v4 . The class-group transfer statement retains the denominator d: Proposition 3.7 works after inverting d or with its stated d-torsion correction. A rational relation alone is not a mod-p upper certificate if p divides d.

3. Etienne, *Computing class groups by induction with generalised norm relations*, https://arxiv.org/html/2411.13124v2 . Generalised norm relations require an actual group-module factorization with the stated scalar. The older C1 file tests a particular modular factorization. Neither its existence nor its arithmetic consequences are inferred from matching rational characters alone.
