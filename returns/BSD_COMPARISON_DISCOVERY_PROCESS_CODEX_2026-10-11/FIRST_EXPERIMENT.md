# First comparison-discovery experiment

**Prepared, not run.** The purpose is to discover a mixed-place comparison mechanism on an actual small arithmetic model. It is not a campaign to certify more individual seed primes, and it does not assume that the same mechanism will work for the seed.

## Question

Can the geometrically normalized CM/Kummer realization and the toric realization be described using one common global residual representation, so that their relative arithmetic content can be measured before choosing separate local scalar bases?

The existing finite-modulus C3 construction is retained. The proposed experiment concerns the later mixed-place comparison. It does not reopen the old, superseded claim that arbitrary-prime results cannot assemble over a finite modulus.

## First deliverable: a diagram that actually exists

1. Fix a small base curve, an admitted first-raised quotient, coefficient modulus, CM field, and second auxiliary place. Verify every local splitting and congruence requirement. Produce both source lattices and their maps, with their kernels, before taking a numerical ratio.
2. Construct a single Galois-equivariant identification of the residual quotient with the base representation. Record addition and Weil-pairing compatibility, allowing an explicit similitude factor. Equality of a few Frobenius traces, or equality of torsion fields alone, does not construct this identification.
3. Use that same identification at both local places. The toric singular line at the raised prime and the good finite-local line at the second prime are not the same module by notation; retain the tensor/Hom-line transport already specified in C6.
4. Apply the existing degree-corrected CM map. Record the full coefficient-valued output rather than replace it with an independently normalized primitive scalar eigenvector.
5. Write the candidate second realization and its dependence on the actual arithmetic input. If it is not available, specify the missing morphism and attempt its construction. Do not populate its values from the conjectural seed identity or from an analytic Sha prediction.

## Historical small candidates, not admitted models

The record suggests base curve 2541c1, coefficient 5, and elliptic raised candidates 287133c1 at 113 and 424347b1 at 167. They are starting candidates only. The recorded short trace scans are not congruence certificates or global torsion-module identifications.

Admission requires the correct common-level stabilized/depleted congruence criterion, an actual representation identification, and a CM field meeting this model's splitting conditions. In particular, do not copy K=Q(sqrt(-7)) blindly: 7 divides 2541. A curve's rank-one raised partner does not transfer a rank-one theorem to the seed.

An earlier pilot's scalar formula also assumed Sha(E/K)[5]=0. An analytic Sha value prime to 5 does not certify that assumption. The model may still serve as an instrumentation test, but any conclusion using that assumption remains conditional unless it is independently proved. No running-time estimate from the old proposal is accepted as a current measurement.

## Measurements that distinguish a mechanism from normalization

Once the maps exist, collect exact outputs for a small number of inputs that test different relations: degree-zero divisor combinations, a permitted Hecke action, a conductor norm, and a second auxiliary choice. Choose held-out comparisons before fitting any scalar. Record the identity used to put outputs in comparable modules, not just coordinates after two unrelated basis choices.

For genuine maps A and B, the tensor cross-difference W in REPORT.md can test proportionality without division. First determine whether its vanishing is already automatic from multiplicity one. If it is, move to the integral index and its geometric source; repeated zero cross-differences add nothing. If it is not, compute the first nonzero discrepancy and how it changes under the selected arithmetic operations.

The desired output is one of: an explicit common-source map; an exact correction identity explaining a discrepancy; or a precise arithmetic boundary map whose image can now be calculated. A list of matching values with a newly fitted unit is insufficient. No formula is presumed to have a constant ratio.

## What would justify the next experiment

- A proved correction formula with fixed denominators: test its dependence on the auxiliary level and then on the coefficient prime.
- A p-dependent but geometric correction: retain its valuation and investigate a correlated bound, rather than demand separate unitness.
- A surviving global defect: identify an arithmetic operation that measures it independently. Merely renaming it Sha, a Kato coordinate, or an unknown scalar does not advance control.
- Failure of the particular model's congruence or higher-precision lift: replace that model or narrow the claim. This is not a nonexistence theorem for a global comparison.

Mod 5 data cannot answer a mod 25 index question without a certified mod 25 model. The finite-source experiment is therefore designed first to learn the correct map and relation, not to report unearned p-adic precision. Full-seed Brandt-module enumeration and large CM sums are not authorized or necessary for this design step.

Only after a source comparison has arithmetic content beyond automatic proportionality should its output be matched to the actual seed derivative or a proven controlling quotient. The final criterion remains delta nonzero and m-w at most one on a cofinite domain; a finite model validates the proposed mechanism, not that uniform conclusion.
