## Why this is not the rejected campaign again

The rejected W3 campaign supplied an extractor consuming two sets of classical higher-weight symbols. This supplement instead defines the finite integral coefficient module carrying a single family source, gives its actual matrix action, and specifies the inhomogeneous eigenlifting system and dual row certificate that would produce source values.

The general idea of a source-specific finite-precision evaluator was already explicitly left open in wall W28. This package instantiates a particular mechanism; it does not claim to have invented that goal. Global eigensymbol reconstruction remains unperformed.

The unit determinant 24 is a local interpolation fact, not a substitute for the old unknown scalar or a proof of primary vanishing. The lifting Jacobian, if invertible, would determine a source but would not force a nonzero functional. The proposed unit-forcing identity remains a research objective.

This construction does not reuse the balanced pencil tails, old cross-member row subtraction, invalid divided norms, rationalized finite augmentation, or a regulator determinant presented as a modular witness. It does not assert that those failures rule out all other methods.

## Relation to published computation methods

Dummit, Hablicsek, Harron, Jain, Pollack and Ross, *Explicit computations of Hida families via overconvergent modular symbols* (Research in Number Theory 2, article 25, 2016; arXiv:1510.05795v2), provide family-symbol computation methods. Their Sections 2–4 supply relevant coefficient, ordinary-symbol and finite-approximation context. The paper explicitly warns that a naive fixed-weight filtration does not automatically work for families.

That literature motivates checking the low-order filtration rather than assuming it. It is not a theorem that this seed's huge-level localized system has an affordable sparse solver, nor a proof that the present measure model has already been identified with the retained optimized Kato source.
