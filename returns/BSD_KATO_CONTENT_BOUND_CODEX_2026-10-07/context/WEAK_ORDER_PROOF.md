# A finiteness-free weak-order deduction

**Grade: DERIVED_FROM_RETAINED_AND_PUBLISHED_INPUTS; independent review pending.**
This is an adaptation of a published proof, not a novelty claim. It is not the full
BKS order-of-vanishing theorem, a regulator comparison, or a BSD bound.

## 1. Statement and conventions

Let E be the retained seed, let p≥5 be a good prime with irreducible E[p], and let
T=T_pE. Put S={∞}∪{primes dividing pN}. Let Q_n be the degree-p^n cyclotomic
layer, Γ=Gal(Q_∞/Q), Λ=Z_p[[Γ]], U=γ−1 and I=(U). Define

    M = lim_cor H¹(O_(Q_n,S),T),
    X = lim_cor H²(O_(Q_n,S),T).

Use the actual integral, smoothed Kato elements z_n={c,d}z_(Q_n) in the
elliptic Tate-module realization fixed in BKS I §2.1. Keep c,d and ξ fixed
along this tower, with BKS's coprimality and congruence conditions. Write
z_∞=(z_n). This statement does not divide away smoothing factors or identify
the modular-parametrization lattice by an unspecified integral unit.

Then

    z_∞ ∈ I M.                                             (W1)

Consequently z_n∈I_n H¹(O_(Q_n,S),T) for every n. There is a unique y_∞∈M
with z_∞=U y_∞, and its augmentation defines an integral derivative

    ∂_γ z = image(y_∞ mod U) ∈ H¹(Z[1/S],T).                 (W2)

Neither a nonzero value of ∂_γ z nor its membership in the rational-point
subspace is asserted. Changing γ changes its first graded coordinate by a
p-adic unit. It is a cyclotomic derivative, not a tame two-prime packet.

No Sha-finiteness hypothesis or ordinarity hypothesis is used below.

## 2. Imported inputs and their matching

1. The retained two independent rational points give an injection
   Z_p²→H¹(Z[1/S],T) by Kummer. No identification of this entire cohomology
   group with Mordell–Weil is made.
2. Global Euler characteristic, with H⁰=0 and complex conjugation having one
   negative eigenvalue, gives h¹−h²=1 for rational S-cohomology.
   See Loeffler–Zerbes, *Euler systems with local conditions*, proof of
   Proposition 2.8. This formula does not assume finite Sha.
3. Cyclotomic derived control gives

       0→M/UM→H¹(Z[1/S],T)→X[U]→0,
       X/UX ≅ H²(Z[1/S],T).                              (W3)

   These are cohomology sequences of multiplication by U on the Iwasawa
   cohomology complex. Cohomology above degree two vanishes (p odd).
   The finite-level base-change convention is BKS I (2.3.1); the inverse-limit
   version follows from the same Shapiro/control construction. This uses
   the full S-cohomology complex, not an unproved Selmer specialization.
4. Kato 12.4 gives rational Iwasawa rank one for M, torsion for X, and
   torsion-freeness in degree one. Kato 12.5(3), at the height-one ideal (U),
   gives

       length_(Λ_(U)) X_(U)
          ≤ length_(Λ_(U))(M_(U)/Λ_(U)z_∞),              (W4)

   when z_∞ is nonzero. If it is zero, (W1) is immediate.
   Kato's inequality first uses the module generated by his zeta map;
   the cyclic module generated by this smoothed element is contained in it
   (12.6), so replacing the former by the latter only increases the quotient
   length. This is an inequality, not an Iwasawa-main-conjecture equality.

Here the Kato representation and finite-character component must be transported
to T_pE as in BKS I (2.1.1) and §4.2. The augmentation U=0 is in that transported
elliptic convention, not an unshifted character in Kato's original notation.
The lattice is homothetic under residual irreducibility; the statement concerns
the actual integral image in the specified lattice.

Kato 12.5(3) includes a local H² correction. Its stated exceptional case requires
failure of potential good reduction at p, and hence is absent here. Passing from
Kato's j_* convention (8.2,12.2) to the S-imprimitive convention does not alter
the length at (U): the extra away-from-p local factors have nonzero augmentation
P_l(l^(-1))=#E^ns(F_l)/l and are units after localization at (U).
They need not be p-units. This is precisely a height-one-(U) comparison,
not permission to discard these factors in a residual calculation.

## 3. Proof

First H¹(Z[1/S],T) is Z_p-torsion-free: its p-torsion is a quotient of
H⁰(Z[1/S],E[p]), which is zero by irreducibility. The first map in (W3)
therefore makes M/UM Z_p-torsion-free.

One may now recover integral freeness rather than assume Sha finite to obtain it.
M is a finite torsion-free Λ-module of rank one, and M_(U) is a free rank-one
module over the DVR Λ_(U). Thus

    (M/UM)⊗Q_p ≅ M_(U)/UM_(U)

has dimension one. Consequently M/UM≅Z_p. Lift its generator to m∈M.
Nakayama applied to M/Λm, since U is in the maximal ideal, gives M=Λm.
Torsion-freeness makes this an isomorphism M≅Λ.

The Kummer inclusion and Euler characteristic imply

    rank_Zp H²(Z[1/S],T)
       = rank_Zp H¹(Z[1/S],T)−1 ≥ 1.                    (W5)

Only a lower bound is used. Unknown Tate-module directions of Sha can increase
the left side and are not discarded.

By (W3), X/UX has Z_p-rank at least one. Write the finite-length DVR module as

    X_(U) ≅ ⊕_(j=1)^b Λ_(U)/(U^(e_j)),    e_j≥1.

Its quotient by U has Q_p-dimension b. Hence

    length X_(U)=Σ e_j ≥ b
       = dim_Qp((X/UX)⊗Q_p) ≥ 1.

Write z_∞=f m in the free module M. By (W4), ord_U(f)≥1. Since f∈Λ,
this means f∈UΛ, not just f∈UΛ_(U): a power series is divisible by U exactly
when its constant coefficient vanishes. This proves (W1).

Division by U is unique because M is torsion-free. Taking the inverse-limit
class y_∞ to level zero gives (W2), using the injection in (W3). Projection
to level n proves the claimed finite-level weak divisibility.

This repeats the length argument behind published BKS I Proposition 4.4, but
replaces its rank equality by (W5). The section containing that proposition
assumes primary finiteness. The deduction here is written separately; it is
not a claim that the printed theorem omitted that assumption.

## 4. Exactly what nonzero derivative would imply

There is a useful conditional consequence. If ∂_γ z≠0 after tensoring with Q_p,
then ord_U(f)=1. Equations (W3)–(W5) and (W4) force h²=1 and h¹=2.
The compact Selmer group lies in H¹, and its Kummer exact sequence is

    0→E(Q)⊗Z_p→Sel_compact(E/Q,T)→T_p Sha(E/Q)→0.

It follows that T_p Sha=0. Since the primary Selmer group is cofinitely
generated over Z_p, this implies Sha[p^∞] is finite.

This proves a conditional primary-finiteness implication. It does NOT prove
Sha[p]=0: a nontrivial finite primary group has zero Tate module. It also
does not make infinitely many finite primary groups into one finite group.

The derivative can vanish. Even when it does not, its p-adic size is
uncontrolled. In the abstract free module Λm, all of

    z=U m,    z=pU m,    z=U²m

satisfy (W1). Their divided augmentations are respectively m, pm and zero.
These are logical controls, not proposed seed Euler systems.

## 5. Scope of the repair

The construction/order step can be attempted without assuming FIN. The next
assertion, identifying this derivative with an analytic Mordell–Weil
Bockstein expression in a matched lattice, is still an arithmetic comparison.
The target cannot be silently restricted to E(Q)⊗Z_p, since the proof used
only its inclusion in H¹. Nor does the cyclotomic result supply tame
regulator content or cross-prime primitivity.

Independent review should check the transported Kato component, the
S-imprimitive height-one comparison, and (W3) before promoting this deduction.
FIN, ORDER9 and Q36 are unchanged.
