## 1. The \(17\)-calculation gives the stated cancellation

Using exact rational arithmetic on \(P_1,P_2\), I reproduced
\[
\frac1{17}
\begin{pmatrix}\log_\omega(P_1)\\ \log_\omega(P_2)\end{pmatrix}
\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}2\\13\end{pmatrix}
\pmod{17}.
\]

With the retained height matrix
\[
H_\gamma\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}13&5\\5&15\end{pmatrix}\pmod{17},
\]
this gives
\[
\frac{\operatorname{adj}(H_\gamma)\log(P)}{17}
\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}16\\6\end{pmatrix}\pmod{17}.
\]
I also recomputed the determinant from the saved higher-precision height digits, obtaining
\[
v_{17}(\det H_\gamma)=1,\qquad
\frac{\det H_\gamma}{17}\equiv13\pmod{17}.
\]
That last check reuses the recorded heights; it is not an independent height computation.  

BKS’s Rubin prefactor is
\[
\epsilon_\rho=(1-\rho^{-1})^{-1}(1-\sigma^{-1}),
\qquad \sigma=17/\rho.
\]
At this ordinary prime its valuation is \(-1\). Consequently, **with the height and source conventions matched**, the coordinate formula indeed gives
\[
\boxed{\min\{v_{17}(A_{17}),v_{17}(B_{17})\}
=v_{17}(K_{17})-1.}
\]
The prefactor is the one in BKS’s generalized Rubin formula; its application without an initial finiteness assumption uses the project’s separate case-split argument. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.07404v2) 

Thus \(K_{17}\equiv0\pmod{17}\) is **forced by integrality**, not evidence that the derivative is nonprimitive. The unresolved test is
\[
\boxed{K_{17}/17\not\equiv0\pmod{17}.}
\]

This explicitly corrects my earlier treatment of the \(17\)-adic regulator as an automatic reason to reject the route.

## 3. The fixed-source normalization argument has the right scope

Fixing one rational geometric map makes its two integral Betti lattices commensurable. Their comparison can therefore introduce nonunit factors at only finitely many primes. A fixed nonzero Betti input and fixed smoothing parameters likewise contribute only fixed finite supports. BKS’s construction explicitly uses these image lattices, Betti inputs and periods. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.07404v2) [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.07404v2)

That supports the return’s cofinite normalization conclusion:
\[
v_p(K_p)=v_p([U^2]L_{\mathrm{std},p})
\]
on the stated matched ordinary domain. It does **not** bound the intrinsic valuation on either side. The integer clearing normalization discrepancies is not a Sha annihilator. 

There is also a specific-prime qualification: **an unspecified finite exceptional set cannot certify that \(17\) lies outside it.** The report correctly leaves the exact source-lattice and numerical-height matching at \(17\) explicit. The proposed fixed smoothing itself causes no problem there—I checked that \(c,d\equiv11,4\pmod{17}\), making its augmentation a unit—but that does not settle the other normalization factors.  

## What is now missing

For nonsingular matched heights, write
\[
r_p=v_p(\det H_p),\qquad
c_p=\min_i v_p\!\left((\operatorname{adj}(H_p)\log(P))_i\right).
\]
On the ordinary domain, integrality already supplies
\[
v_p(K_p)\ge r_p-c_p+1.
\]
**Primitivity requires equality.** The missing input is an arithmetic upper estimate excluding an additional factor of \(p\), not another proof of integrality or a demand that the regulator be a unit. Singular heights and supersingular primes still require their separately stated versions; the ordinary formula cannot replace them. 

So this return does two identifiable things: it bounds **normalization exceptions** by a fixed finite support, and it verifies on the actual seed why **correlated divisibility**, rather than coefficient unitness, is the correct test. It does not yet show that the test succeeds at \(17\) or cofinitely. **FIN, ORDER9 and Q36 remain unchanged.**
