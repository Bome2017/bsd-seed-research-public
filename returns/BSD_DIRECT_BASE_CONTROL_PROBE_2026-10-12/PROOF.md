# A norm-kernel injection into a finite character object

2026-10-12. Grade: DERIVED under the named inputs. No novelty claim. The independent arithmetic bound in Section 5 is not proved.

## 1. Precise hypotheses and sources

Let E/Q be the retained rank-two seed. Fix p>=5 and use E(Q)[p]=0, supplied by its retained rational torsion calculation. Let L/Q be cyclic of degree p with G=<sigma>. Choose a faithful character chi of G. Suppose its central twist value L(E,chi,1) is nonzero.

THEOREM (imported): Kato, Asterisque 295 (2004), Theorem 14.2(2) and Corollary 14.3, printed page 235, for a modular abelian quotient over an abelian extension, gives finiteness of the full integral character part of the Selmer group when the corresponding central value is nonzero. The character part is the subgroup annihilated by the kernel of Z[G] -> Qbar. The primary source and all-prime nature of this assertion were checked in the pinned RANK_ZERO_SOURCE_CHECK.md. This probe retains that source check rather than claiming a fresh full-PDF reading.

For a cyclic prime-degree group and faithful chi this ideal is generated by

\[
N_G=1+\sigma+\cdots+\sigma^{p-1}.
\]

Write A=E[p^\infty], S_Q=Sel_{p^\infty}(E/Q), S_L=Sel_{p^\infty}(E/L), and

\[
K=\ker(N_G:S_L\to S_L).
\]

Thus K is a finite p-primary group, with module structure over the DVR

\[
\mathcal O_p=\mathbf Z_p[G]/(N_G)\simeq\mathbf Z_p[\zeta_p],
\qquad \pi=\sigma-1.
\]

The residue field is F_p and the valuation here has v_pi(pi)=1; it is not v_p(p)=1. In particular length_O K=log_p(#K).

This construction can be specified, for example, in the degree-p subfield of Q(mu_(p^2)), or in the degree-p subfield of Q(mu_ell) for a prime ell=1 mod p. These descriptions do not prove the required twist nonvanishing. No first-layer nonvanishing for all p is asserted.

## 2. Restriction is injective before any point-rank comparison

DERIVED: A(L)=0. Indeed, E[p](L) is a finite-dimensional F_p representation of the p-group G. If nonzero, it has a nonzero invariant vector. For this cyclic group, this follows directly from (sigma-1)^p=sigma^p-1=0: a nilpotent endomorphism on a nonzero vector space has nonzero kernel. An invariant would be a rational p-torsion point. Higher p-power torsion would yield p-torsion, so it is absent as well.

Inflation-restriction now makes

\[
H^1(\mathbf Q,A)\longrightarrow H^1(L,A)
\]

injective, with image in the G-invariants. Local Kummer conditions are preserved by restriction, giving an injection S_Q -> S_L. No assertion about surjectivity of a Selmer control map is made.

For s in S_Q[p], its restricted class is invariant, so

\[
N_G\operatorname{res}(s)=p\operatorname{res}(s)=0,
\qquad (\sigma-1)\operatorname{res}(s)=0.
\]

Consequently

\[
S_Q[p]\hookrightarrow K[\pi].                                      \tag{1}
\]

Conversely K[pi] is killed by p, since N_G acts as p on invariants. It is therefore an F_p-vector space. An equality in (1) would require a further check of local descent conditions; this proof neither needs nor asserts it.

## 3. Remove exactly the known base points

Let W_p=E(Q) tensor Q_p/Z_p. The retained rank-two calculation gives W_p=(Q_p/Z_p)^2, with W_p[p]=F_p^2. The Kummer sequence is

\[
0\longrightarrow W_p\longrightarrow S_Q\longrightarrow
\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[p^\infty]\longrightarrow0.
\]

Multiplication by p on W_p is surjective. Taking p-torsion therefore gives the exact sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow W_p[p]\longrightarrow S_Q[p]\longrightarrow
\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[p]\longrightarrow0.                              \tag{2}
\]

This does not assume that the primary Sha group is finite. The image of W_p[p] under (1) is exactly a two-dimensional point plane. Quotienting the injective map (1) by that same plane gives the canonical injection

\[
\boxed{\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[p]\hookrightarrow
K[\pi]/\operatorname{res}(W_p[p]).}                                \tag{3}
\]

This map is defined via Selmer p-torsion modulo the point plane. It does NOT require restriction on Sha itself to be injective. Confusing those two assertions would introduce an unjustified capitulation argument.

No use was made of E(L)=E(Q). Point growth, if present in another setting where K was not known finite, would not invalidate the formal injection. The twist nonvanishing is used for finiteness of K, not to erase the point plane.

## 4. A sufficient upper bound, not an obtained one

A finite module over O_p decomposes as direct sum O_p/(pi^{a_i}), with a_i>=1. Thus

\[
\dim_{\mathbf F_p}K[\pi]=\#\{i\}\le\sum_i a_i
=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_p}K.
\]

Equations (2)-(3) give

\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[p]
\le\dim_{\mathbf F_p}K[\pi]-2
\le\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_p}K-2.}                         \tag{4}
\]

In particular an independent proof that length_O K<=2 forces K[pi] to be exactly the point plane and Sha[p]=0. A nonzero p-primary torsion group always has an element of order p, so Sha[p^infinity]=0 follows. No alternating-pairing argument or preliminary primary finiteness is necessary for this threshold.

If t is an authenticated nonzero element of Fitt_{O_p,0}(K), then length_O K<=v_pi(t). Therefore v_pi(t)<=2 would be an alternative certificate. This statement requires membership in the ACTUAL zeroth Fitting ideal. Identifying a symbol, a character value, or a rationally normalized Euler-system scalar with such an element is a separate integral arithmetic theorem. It is not provided by the definition of K or by Kato's finiteness corollary.

In particular this proof does not assert

    tau(chi) L(E,chi^-1,1)/Omega_E belongs to Fitt_0(K).

Its truth in a specified setting requires source and local-condition work not supplied here. It must not be presumed just because the displayed central value is nonzero.

## 5. Test against the existing project, rather than promote a restatement

At a coefficient prime q different from p, multiplication by p is invertible on q-primary groups. Integral averaging separates the invariant and faithful components. That is the separation in the reviewed quintic calculation at q=19. At coefficient p, (3) demonstrates why the corresponding separation is unavailable and why the norm-kernel can detect base p-torsion.

This change supplies a genuine map, but it supplies no numerical upper bound for its target. The simplest scalar attempt does not produce a new arithmetic premise: the project already uses the tame rank-two leading-symbol nonvanishing criterion in Ota Theorem 1.5/6.4. Its ambient requirements are the relevant integrality ring, non-CM curve, good/non-anomalous coefficient prime, Tamagawa units, full p-adic image, p>=rank, and square-free good auxiliary conductor with the specified cyclic residual local groups. Under them nonzero leading augmentation class implies Sha[p]=0. The statement and ambient hypotheses were freshly checked on Ota's printed pages 2-3 and the text of Section 6.

This probe has NOT proved equivalence of the length bound (4) with Ota's premise. It has NOT derived a stronger integral character bound, a new symbol value, or cofinite nonvanishing. Replacing a missing derivative estimate by an uncomputed length of K is not, by itself, progress through the seed's upper-bound step.

There is an elementary control on the specific finiteness-only inference. Take an abstract discrete module

\[
S=(\mathbf Q_p/\mathbf Z_p)^{2+2a},\qquad
W=(\mathbf Q_p/\mathbf Z_p)^2,
\]

with trivial G-action and any a>=1. Its norm kernel is S[p], which is finite of length 2+2a, while S/W is infinite. The nontrivial integral character quotient S/N_GS is zero. This is NOT an elliptic-curve realization or an obstruction theorem for Kato's full Euler system. It verifies only that character-part finiteness, the point plane, and even excess corank do not algebraically give the missing upper bound.
