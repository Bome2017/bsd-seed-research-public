# The actual two-prime target and its normalization

## Domain and imports

Fix a good seed prime p>=5 and put k=F_p. Retain the v0.2.6 seed hypotheses and the stated imported-theorem grades of B.20--B.24. No source theorem is promoted to a human or Lean certificate here.

Let ell_1 != ell_2 be primes not dividing Np such that

    ell_i = 1 mod p,       a_(ell_i) = ell_i+1 = 2 mod p.

The cyclic admissible subfamily additionally has nonidentity unipotent residual Frobenius, equivalently the local p-torsion has dimension one. The group-algebra calculations below work for either family. Whenever the cyclic family is required, use it throughout; no identity-Frobenius prime is silently substituted. The retained minimum-witness and semilocal results supply a cyclic pair when a unit pair exists.

Choose primitive roots g_i and x_i(a)=log_(g_i)(a) modulo p. Set n=ell_1 ell_2. In Kim's complete-sum, total-real-period convention,

    delta_n = sum_(a in (Z/n)^*) [a/n]^+ x_1(a)x_2(a) in k.       (T1)

This is the formula in Kim, arXiv:2203.12159v6, section 1.4. The same source's section 1.2.2 fixes the prime set and I_n. Primitive-root changes scale (T1) by units; this does not prove it nonzero.

## Homology source and the factor of two

Use the retained period functional z=z_E, with Ota real-projection period Omega_O, and Manin factor c_E dividing 512. The total real period in (T1) is Omega^+=2 Omega_O for this seed. The actual closed cusp cycle is

    C_(a,n)={infinity,a/n}-{infinity,0} in H_1(X_0(N),Z).

The cusps a/n and 0 agree on X_0(N), and the rank-two input gives L(E,1)=0. Thus

    z(C_(a,n))=2c_E [a/n]^+.                              (T2)

Accordingly the complete mixed-weight cycle K_(ell_1,ell_2)=sum_a x_1(a)x_2(a)C_(a,n) satisfies

    z(K_(ell_1,ell_2))=eta delta_n,       eta=2c_E in k^*. (T3)

Integers representing x_i may be chosen arbitrarily modulo p. This formula does not replace complete sums with representative sums modulo sign, and does not discard Euler or period factors.

## Exact mixed quotient

Let G=C_p x C_p with generators sigma,tau, put X=sigma-1, Y=tau-1, and

    R=k[G]=k[X,Y]/(X^p,Y^p),     J=(X,Y).

The full evaluated packet is

    Theta_n=sum_a [a/n]^+ sigma^(x_1(a))tau^(x_2(a)).

Its XY coefficient modulo J^3 is exactly delta_n, by the binomial expansion. On the retained domain the unconditional calculation behind B.24 gives

    Theta_n = delta_n XY mod J^3.                         (T4)

For completeness: the rank-two/Fitting input places the full packet and each one-prime projection in augmentation degree at least two. The Betti norm factor at the other Kato prime is

    2-u-u^(-1)=-(u-1)^2/u.

It raises the degree of a one-prime packet to at least four. Projection therefore forces both pure quadratic coefficients of Theta_n to be zero; constant and linear coefficients were zero already. This argument does not assume delta_n is a unit.

The mixed quotient in the request is

    M=I_1 I_2/(I_1^2 I_2+I_1 I_2^2) = k XY.

For arbitrary elements of R, a well-defined mixed projection is obtained by

    Q(F)=F(X,Y)-F(X,0)-F(0,Y)+F(0,0),

then passing to M. It extracts the XY coefficient. On (T4) it gives delta_n XY.

## Endpoint, with its logical strength stated exactly

I_p^(2) is the ideal of k generated by (T1) for all admissible pairs, with finite sums understood. Since k is a field,

    -2 in I_p^(2)
    <=> some delta_n != 0
    <=> r_p=2
    <=> Sha(E/Q)[p^infinity]=0.                           (T5)

The latter arrows are retained B.20--B.21, not new conclusions of this pass. Proving an equivalent statement independently is a valid route. But using ideal membership rather than identifying one cycle removes a choice of pair; it does not lower the arithmetic strength of the requested theorem.

No assertion is made that the ideal in (T5) is the whole field until an independent comparison supplies that membership.
