# Review of the independent companion S-class bound

2026-10-15. Separate same-team mathematical review. This is a written deduction and source screen, not a formal proof or an independent replay of the BNF certifications. It does not settle a seed endpoint.

## Verdict

The proposed p=3 bound is mathematically stronger than controlling the homology of the two residue norm matrices. Under the retained full-image and actual integral incidence-map inputs, it bounds the entire global source-admitted space, including its unit and ideal-class parts:

    dim_F3 K_A <= 1 + t_D + c_B,S^- - (c_A,S^- - r_A,S^-)
               <= 1 + t_D + c_B,S^-.

Here K_A is the scalar-compatible kernel of the actual affine norm on A(S,3), c is 3-adic class-group length, r is class-group 3-rank, and t_D is an integral index on the primitive S-divisor lattice. It is not a restatement using a Selmer dimension. Its arithmetic inputs are class groups, prime ideals and incidence norms. The numerical input t_D must not be computed from an already known dimension of K_A.

The companion calculation now evaluates these inputs on the 389a1/mirror pair. Its resulting bounds 2 and 3 agree with the previously certified whole global norm kernels. This is a fixed-prime arithmetic upper-bound mechanism, demonstrated on controls. It has not been bounded across coefficient primes or applied independently to the missing seed tail.

## Definitions required for the bound

Let A be the nonzero 3-torsion point field and B the nonzero dual-vector/affine-line field. Let tau be the internal scalar -1 involution. Full GL_2(F_3) image makes both degree eight fields with signature (2,3); neither contains mu_3. Superscript minus means the tau=-1 summand after tensoring with Z_3, using the integral idempotent (1-tau)/2.

Use the same rational prime set S on both fields. To contain the actual Selmer image, it must contain the relevant descent ramification support. It cannot be reduced to a convenient subset merely to improve the bound.

Let U_A and U_A,S be the ordinary and S-unit free Z_3 lattices, respectively. Let D_A,S be the free Z_3 group on all primes of A above S, and Lambda_A the image of S-unit valuations. Use the primitive minus lattice D_A,S^-, not Lambda_A, as the divisor-index source. Put

    c_A = length_Z3 Cl(A)[3^infinity]^-,
    c_A,S = length_Z3 Cl(O_A[1/S])[3^infinity]^-,
    r_A,S = dim_F3 Cl(O_A[1/S])[3]^-,

and similarly for B. Set

    s_U = length coker(u:U_A^- -> U_B^-),
    s_S = length coker(u:U_A,S^- -> U_B,S^-),
    t_D = length coker(u:D_A,S^- -> D_B,S^-).

These cokernels are finite and killed by three because the literal transpose incidence maps satisfy vu=uv=3 on minus lattices. Thus s_U and s_S also equal the dimensions of their kernels modulo three. This statement uses actual saturated Z_3 lattices, not a finite-index guessed set of generators.

## Derivation with all finite indices

The equivariant S-unit/class exact sequences give

    0 -> U_A^- -> U_A,S^- -> Lambda_A -> 0,
    0 -> Lambda_A -> D_A,S^- -> C_A,S -> 0,

where C_A,S is the minus subgroup generated by the S-prime classes. Consequently

    length C_A,S = c_A-c_A,S.

The incidence map commutes with valuations. Determinants on these exact lattice sequences therefore give

    s_S = s_U + t_D + (c_A-c_A,S) - (c_B-c_B,S).       (1)

For clarity, the plus sign on the A correction is essential. If Lambda_A has larger index inside its primitive divisor lattice, its image under a fixed map has the correspondingly larger index in Lambda_B.

The retained ordinary-unit index argument yields, at coefficient three,

    s_U = 1 + c_B-c_A.                                (2)

It uses equality of the point/covector permutation characters, the number-field analytic class-number formula and the real incidence determinant of absolute value three. The common quartic plus field cancels. This is the usual number-field class-number theorem; it does not invoke BSD or an elliptic L-value. Combining (1) and (2) gives

    s_S = 1+t_D+c_B,S-c_A,S.                          (3)

Finally the exact Kummer sequence is

    0 -> U_A,S/U_A,S^3 -> A(S,3) -> Cl(O_A[1/S])[3] -> 0.

Taking the minus part is exact. The intersection of K_A with the first term has dimension s_S: an S-unit whose affine norm is a cube in B has an S-unit cube root, because every valuation outside S of that root is zero. The projection of K_A to the last term has dimension at most r_A,S. This proves the displayed bound, retaining class-group exponents through the nonnegative difference c_A,S-r_A,S.

No assertion that the class projection is surjective is needed. In fact class lifting obstructions can make the bound strict. Local Selmer conditions may make it smaller still.

## Why the paired residue complex alone did not suffice

If J is the opposite norm image, a small ker(u)/J controls only the quotient after J. It does not bound admitted classes inside J. The S-unit index formula instead retains the index of the actual unit lattice and bounds all of ker(u), including J. This is the substantive difference. None of it says that the torsion field or its class numbers are independent of the coefficient prime.

## Certification and endpoint boundary

A fresh exact computation of t_D can use principal approximation elements with prescribed valuations, literal inclusion/norm in the incidence algebra, and target valuations. Such elements need not be S-units globally: their valuations at the chosen S-primes determine the divisor map, since distinct rational prime supports do not mix under the correspondence. The primitive anti basis and both involutions must be checked. A matrix of already admitted source values cannot substitute for this construction.

The saved BNF class-group certifications retain their prior grades. This review did not rerun them or the divisor producer. It checked the mathematical index passage and the numerical evaluation of the displayed independent inputs. FIN, ORDER9 and Q36 remain unchanged. The bound supplies a concrete arithmetic object to estimate; the required small estimates for the seed's unresolved coefficient primes are not proved.
