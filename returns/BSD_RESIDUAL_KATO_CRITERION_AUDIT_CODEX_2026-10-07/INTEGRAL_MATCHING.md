## 3. Integral image on the cyclotomic kernel

The retained seed image is GL₂(F_p), not merely irreducible. Here is the
lifting argument needed to apply the stronger integral theorem.

Let G⊂GL₂(Z_p) be the compact image on T_pE, and H the closure of its
commutator subgroup. Then H⊂SL₂(Z_p), and its reduction is SL₂(F_p).
Indeed the commutator subgroup of GL₂(F_p) is SL₂(F_p) for p≥5.

For a lift x of I+E₁₂ modulo p, write x=I+E₁₂+pA modulo p². Expansion
with one occurrence of pA gives

    x^p=(I+E₁₂)^p+
          p∑_(i=0)^(p−1)(I+E₁₂)^i A(I+E₁₂)^(p−1−i)
          =I+pE₁₂  (mod p²).                           (I3)

The correction vanishes because its entries are polynomials of degree at
most two in i, and the sums of 1,i,i² are zero modulo p for p≥5. Conjugates
of E₁₂ span sl₂(F_p): a Weyl element gives E₂₁, and conjugation by
[[1,0],[1,1]] supplies the trace-zero diagonal direction. Consequently
the first congruence kernel of H is all sl₂(F_p).

For n≥1, p-th powering on successive principal congruence layers satisfies

    (I+p^n A)^p=I+p^(n+1)A (mod p^(n+2)).

Induction gives every congruence layer, hence H=SL₂(Z_p) by closedness.
Since det ρ_E,p=χ_cyc, this SL₂ image is the image of elements in
ker χ_cyc=G_(Q(μ_p∞)). The unipotent matrix I+E₁₂ gives the required
rank-one free cokernel. The stronger big-image condition is therefore
satisfied on D (indeed for all retained p≥5).

On the cyclotomic kernel the Tate twist is trivial. Thus it does not alter
this condition when passing between Kato's cohomological representation
and the elliptic realization. A homothety of the entire lattice also
preserves it.

This is a written lifting proof. The small controls for p=5,7 and the
countercontrol at p=3 check (I3), not the seed's global residual theorem.
