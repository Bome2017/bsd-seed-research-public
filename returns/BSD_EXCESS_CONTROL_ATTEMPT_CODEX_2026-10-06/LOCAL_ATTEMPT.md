Source actually inspected: E. Da Ronche, M. Longo, S. Vigni,
*Higher Fitting ideals and the structure of anticyclotomic Shafarevich–Tate groups*,
[arXiv:2603.12357v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12357), 12 March 2026.
The archive had listed this source as title-level unchecked. This pass read the
statements and relevant arguments rather than treating its title as an input.

Source finding (scoped): the ordinary hypotheses are on printed p. 2.
Theorems A/B and 5.9/5.10 identify higher Fitting ideals up to height-two
factors. Corollary 5.4 requires an augmentation specialization outside its
exceptional set. Proposition 5.8 produces a unit after successive auxiliary
primes lower the residual Selmer rank; it does not bound the number of steps
independently of that rank. See printed pp. 3, 38–41. These statements do not
themselves give a small-index augmentation unit uniformly as the coefficient
prime varies. Seed-specific hypotheses and the augmentation exception have
not been discharged in this pass. Supersingular primes are outside the
stated ordinary application.

Attempted construction: use a small higher Fitting ideal of the anticyclotomic
Selmer dual, specialize it at the trivial character, and map its resulting
annihilator to the original excess. The first unsupported arithmetic arrow
is the passage from the Fitting description to a uniformly controlled
augmentation ideal. Neither a bound on its valuation nor a uniform bound on
the necessary auxiliary length was constructed. Thus this application is
not admitted as a new BSD route yet. The paper is not refuted, nor are deeper
uses of its arithmetic ideals excluded.

## Exact algebra check on that attempted specialization

This calculation is ours, and is only an abstract module control. It is not
a seed Selmer example or a counterexample to the cited paper.

Put R=Z_p[[T]]. For every integer e>=0 define

    Y_e = R + (R/(T))^2 + (R/(T+p))^(2e).

Then Y_e has R-rank one and its torsion submodule is exactly a double M+M.
Every torsion summand has Iwasawa mu-invariant zero. There is no nonzero
finite R-submodule: all displayed summands are torsion-free as Z_p-modules.
Nevertheless

    Y_e / T Y_e = Z_p^3 + F_p^(2e).

Indeed R/(T+p) is Z_p with T acting as -p, so its T-coinvariants are F_p.
The residual finite part can therefore have arbitrary even dimension while
all those coarse module properties hold. A characteristic/Fitting
description needs arithmetic information about its actual generators;
generic rank, mu=0, and doubling alone do not provide it.

There is a second distinction: R/(p,T) is pseudo-null and is lost under
pseudo-isomorphism, but its augmentation specialization is F_p. Consequently
a statement modulo height-two errors must not silently be used as an exact
integral specialization. Even removing that error would not remove the first
example's unbounded specialization torsion.
