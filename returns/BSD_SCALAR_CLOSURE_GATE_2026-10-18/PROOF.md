# What the scalar-closure gate proves

This is a finite-family argument using the retained actual point field A, its full integer ring, and scalar automorphism sigma=[3] of order six. Write tau=sigma^3=[-1], and beta_i=alpha_i/tau(alpha_i) for the twenty frozen elements. The original exact ideal factorizations are retained computer-arithmetic inputs; this run does not establish a new maximal order or residual-image theorem.

## The space tested

Let G be the image in A*/A*7 of the multiplicative subgroup generated by sigma^j(beta_i), for i=1,...,20 and j=0,...,5. This definition includes all integer products of all six scalar translates, but does not enlarge the subgroup by extracting new seventh roots outside it.

Let P be the finite prime-ideal support closed under sigma, and let div_7:G -> F7^P be the valuation map modulo seven. An ordinary unit has zero image. The larger ordinary virtual-unit group also has zero image: its valuations are all multiples of seven. This does not say that every virtual unit is an ordinary unit; its ordinary class-group contribution remains possible.

Use the faithful character chi(sigma)=3 matched to the existing evaluator. Since six is invertible modulo seven, its idempotent is

    e_chi = (1/6) sum_{j=0}^5 3^(-j) sigma^j.

The exponent coefficients modulo seven are [6,2,3,1,5,4]. Divisor projection is equivariant when the row permutation is the actual action on ideals. Scalar translates of any beta_i project to multiples of e_chi[beta_i]. Therefore e_chi G is generated by twenty classes, and its divisor image is generated by the twenty columns e_chi div_7(beta_i).

If this matrix has rank twenty, consider any x in e_chi G with div_7(x)=0. Write x=sum_i a_i e_chi[beta_i]. Independence of the twenty divisor columns gives a_i=0 for every i, hence x=0. Thus

    e_chi G intersect ker(div_7) = 0.

In particular, this entire scalar-generated family contains no nonzero class in the faithful ordinary-unit component being tested. This conclusion already controls all the scalar translates. It is stronger than testing only the twenty unprojected relative ratios.

The rank gate is on divisor columns of nonunits. It is not the norm/local rejection rank on ordinary units and cannot be inserted as r_U in the whole-source bound.

## If the rank drops

A kernel vector modulo seven produces a product with valuations divisible by seven. It need not give a divisor equal to zero. It may carry an ordinary ideal-class contribution. To export ordinary units one must instead establish exact integer divisor cancellation and authenticate the product and inverse in the maximal order. The tautological relation tau(beta_i)=beta_i^-1 produces the identity and supplies no unit class. A virtual-unit relation requiring a seventh root or a principalization must retain that additional arithmetic obligation.

Only after authentication and a nonzero faithful projection may the unchanged norm/local evaluator measure a useful rank. Rank two there would yield a whole-unit kernel upper bound two on this rank-two control, under the retained dimension-four theorem. It would not remove the compatible ordinary class term or prove any bound for the original seed across coefficient primes.

## Stronger consequence of the full integer divisor rank

For each i, sigma^(j+3)(beta_i)=sigma^j(beta_i)^(-1). Thus the last three divisor columns of its six-column block are negatives of the first three. The full rank60 forces the sixty first-half columns (three per generator) to be independent over Q. In any integer divisor relation the exponent difference between each paired column must therefore vanish. Every such relation is a product of the exact inverse-pair identities, so its actual field product is1.

Consequently, under the saved factorization inputs and the computed full rank, the entire multiplicative subgroup generated by all120translates contains no ordinary unit other than1. This is an exact finite-family statement, stronger than the matched faithful modulo-seven exclusion. It does not assert that the field has no other units.

## Seventh-root saturation of this multiplicative family

Let gamma_j, j=1,...,60, be the first three translates of each beta_i. The full scalar-generated subgroup H is generated by these elements. The first-half divisor matrix has rank60 modulo7. If x^7=prod_j gamma_j^(a_j) with x in A*, reduction of divisors modulo7 forces every a_j to be divisible by7. Write a_j=7b_j. Then (x/prod_j gamma_j^(b_j))^7=1. The retained field has signature(6,21), hence a real embedding and no nontrivial seventh root of unity. It follows that x=prod_j gamma_j^(b_j), so x lies in H. Iteration gives the same statement for seventh-power towers.

Thus H is7-saturated in A*: adjoining existing field seventh roots of its elements cannot rescue this family. This is a consequence of the measured divisor rank, not an assumed saturation theorem. It does not apply to other generators or arbitrary units in A.

## Scope of a full-rank result

A full-rank result excludes this particular enlarged family. It does not exclude undiscovered units, other element families, or arbitrary principal ideals. The separate saturation deduction excludes seventh-root enlargement of this particular subgroup. The unrestricted relative-unit coverage lemma remains valid but gives no effective size bound. FIN, ORDER9 and Q36 do not follow from this exclusion.
