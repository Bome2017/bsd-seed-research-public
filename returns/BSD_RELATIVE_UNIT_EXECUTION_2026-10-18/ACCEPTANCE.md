# What an output establishes

A candidate is not admitted merely because its relative norm is one. For a searched alpha and integral ideal I, independently verify I is stable under tau, alpha belongs to I, the absolute field norm of alpha has absolute value Norm(I), and the principal ideal equals I. Then epsilon=alpha/tau(alpha) is an ordinary unit. Its exact nontriviality and full scalar-character projection remain separate checks.

The relative norm-one restriction loses no faithful unit classes modulo seven: tau acts as -1 there, so u/tau(u) induces multiplication by2. This is a statement about the unrestricted unit group, not completeness of any finite candidate set. Conversely a norm-one unit epsilon other than -1 is realized by alpha=1+epsilon with stable integral ideal (alpha); this supplies no small bound for the ideal or alpha.

For each accepted family, use the existing split-prime incidence-norm rows and the coefficient-seven formal-local seventh-power condition. A rank r in actual images of authenticated units is a lower bound on the rank of the map on the full four-dimensional faithful unit source, hence gives unit-kernel upper bound 4-r. A zero rank on a found family gives no smaller upper bound. Generator counts and floating logarithmic independence are not replacements for this finite-field rank test.

This is the rank-two -47 twist control. Even an attained unit upper bound two would leave the complete ordinary class term c-r_C in the root formula from the preceding return. Neither unit completeness nor class completeness is assumed from this search. No conclusion for the original BSD seed or across coefficient primes follows automatically.

No stock norm-equation call is used as a purported relative-unit generator. No known Selmer/Sha value is fed into candidate selection or acceptance.
