## 2. The valid classical bookkeeping

Let \(e_p=\dim_{\mathbf F_p}\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[p]\). Retain the saturated rank-two basis, residual hypotheses, the seed root number +1, \(s_p^-=1\), and the nonzero residual Kolyvagin system with Zhang Lemma 8.4 applicable. Define \(\nu_p\) to be its least residual nonzero support length.

The lemma's even case would give \(s_p^-=\nu_p+1\) and \(s_p^+\le\nu_p\). Thus \(\nu_p=0\), contradicting \(s_p^+=2+e_p\ge2\). Therefore \(\nu_p\) is odd, and the odd case gives

\[
2+e_p=s_p^+=\nu_p+1,\qquad e_p=\nu_p-1.
\]

It follows that \(e_p\le1\), \(e_p=0\), and \(\nu_p=1\) are equivalent on this domain. This uses the actual sign and residual theorem; nontriviality at some p-power level alone is insufficient to assert a residual nonzero singleton. None of the equalities bounds \(\nu_p\) independently. This was already retained bookkeeping, not new arithmetic in the returned run.

## 3. Why item 4 is not established

Use \(u\) for the Iwasawa variable and \(\mathbb T=T_pE\), avoiding the return's use of T for both.

### Source/object mismatch

Zhang §1(xii),(xiv) explicitly distinguishes classical Kolyvagin primes from Bertolini–Darmon admissible primes. The former satisfy \(\ell\equiv-1\pmod p\), \(a_\ell\equiv0\pmod p\); the latter require \(\ell^2-1\) invertible mod p. These prime sets are disjoint. This elementary distinction is already visible before comparing any periods or local conditions.

For \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-23})\), every bad prime splits, so \(N^-=1\). In the higher-Fitting paper's bipartite indexing, one-prime entries are scalars \(\lambda_\ell\), whereas \(\kappa_n\) has even support. The return instead declares a first ideal generated by one-prime \(\kappa_\ell^\infty\) and assigns it the higher-Fitting paper's conclusion. It supplies no map between the two systems. We are not asserting that no comparison can exist; the one needed here has not been proved.

The source's even-index ideals \(D_i\) use all permitted linear evaluations of classes with support length at most i. The return's unqualified “coordinates” are not an adequate substitute for this integral content. A choice of coordinate or a residual functional is not automatically an integral dual functional before specialization.

### Exact algebra showing the content gap

This is an abstract algebra control, not a seed Selmer example. Let

\[
R=\mathbf Z_p[[u]],\quad\mathfrak m=(p,u),\quad M=\mathfrak m,\quad x=p\in M.
\]

The class of x in \(M/\mathfrak mM\) is nonzero: p and u form a basis of \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\). Yet every R-linear map f:M→R satisfies

\[
u f(p)=p f(u).
\]

Reducing modulo p and using that u is not a zero divisor in \(\mathbf F_p[[u]]\) gives \(f(p)=pa\) for some a∈R. Cancellation of p then gives \(f(u)=ua\). Hence \(\{f(x):f\in\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)\}=pR\). Its residual content is zero although the residual class is nonzero. M is torsion-free of rank one.

Thus the inference “nonzero residual class implies unit specialized integral content” needs actual local freeness, dual base change, and matching of the class, or another adequate proof. Those properties are not supplied by the return. If a particular arithmetic module has the extra properties, this example does not refute that module; they must be proved for it.

### What the derived sequence does prove

For a perfect complex C of amplitude [1,2] over R, the multiplication-by-u triangle gives

\[
0\to H^1(C)/u\to H^1(C\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}R/u)
\to H^2(C)[u]\to0,
\qquad
H^2(C\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}R/u)\simeq H^2(C)/u.
\]

These are correct formal identities. Identifying this specialized complex with the precise integral Greenberg Selmer complex at finite level requires a source theorem with its local hypotheses. The return names Nekovář but does not check them. Global residual irreducibility alone is not a written verification of every local control or integral dual-base-change assertion. In particular this sequence does not repair the changed Euler-system definitions.

**Verdict on Proposition 1:** retain items 1–3 with the above hypotheses; withhold item 4 and the claimed exact A1→A2 reduction.

## 4. The direct nonexceptional augmentation corollary cannot apply here

This is a scoped deduction from the source's actual corollary, not a new upper bound. Fix p for which that source's ordinary hypotheses hold. Assume the usual Kummer inclusion of \(E(K)\otimes\mathbf Q_p/\mathbf Z_p\) in its finite-level Greenberg Selmer group. With \(N^-=1\), its nonexceptional specialization has corank one (DLRV Corollary 5.4, e=1).

If augmentation \((u)\) were nonexceptional, the corollary would therefore give

\[
\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbf Z_p}\operatorname{Sel}_{\rm Gr}(K,E[p^\infty])=1.
\]

But the Kummer image of the seed's two independent rational points already forces this corank to be at least two. The retained −23 certificate gives the stronger lower bound three. Contradiction. Thus augmentation belongs to the exceptional set for this proposed application, whenever the stated source/local hypotheses are met.

The Kummer inclusion is the standard one: at primes away from p the p-divisible local Kummer image satisfies the propagated finite/unramified condition, and at good ordinary p it satisfies the ordinary condition. Only an inclusion and a corank lower bound are used. No finite-Sha hypothesis for the seed is used.

This strengthens the previous package's “augmentation exception not discharged” into a specific failure of the **direct nonexceptional corollary**. It does not refute the higher-Fitting theorem, force the whole Euler system to vanish, or exclude a separately constructed singular-specialization argument. Such an argument needs additional arithmetic information; the returned four-way equivalence does not supply it.

## 5. Correct the A3 exclusion

First, the quoted leading-term formula is not independently established in the return. An expression involving \(\#\Sha[p^\infty]\) requires finiteness or a precisely specified quotient, and the order-of-vanishing assertion has its own hypotheses. The exact source theorem and these dependencies must be checked before using the displayed expression to prove finiteness. We have not performed that complete source audit here and do not promote the inequality.

Even if its intended inequality holds on a properly justified domain, put

\[
a_p=v_p(L_p^*),\quad b_p=v_p(R_p/p^2),\quad c_p=v_p(\text{local factor}).
\]

The proposed sufficient estimate is a bound on \(a_p-b_p-c_p\), not necessarily \(b_p=0\). For example the abstract valuation pattern \(a_p=b_p=5,c_p=0\) yields difference zero with a nonunit regulator. This is a logical control, not data about this seed. Independently proving a correlated valuation bound would also suffice. No such proof is present, but it would be wrong to exclude it merely because regulator-unit control is unavailable.

The user's prohibition is against assuming the uncontrolled regulator property or a conjectural BSD normalization. It does not prohibit a genuinely proved integral comparison. None was constructed in this return.

## 6. Correct the terminology

For a fixed p, \(\Sha[p]\) is finite as a quotient of the finite p-Selmer group. That is not the cofinite target. The missing assertion is vanishing outside one finite set of coefficient primes, together with the separate remaining primary-finiteness obligations for FIN. We do not repeat the return's literal statement that finiteness of \(\Sha[p]\) cofinitely is an open problem, nor make a new universal literature claim about all rank-two curves.

An identification of the receiver or short deformation quotient with Sha remains a valid target identification under its retained inputs. It does not prohibit independent arithmetic estimates on that object. Nothing in this review establishes that all such estimates are impossible.
